The inverter is the brain of a rooftop solar system. Panels make DC electricity; the inverter converts it to the AC your home and the grid use, and it decides how your system behaves during shading, power cuts and grid export. Panels get the attention, but the inverter choice often does more to shape day-to-day experience - and it is where a good installer earns their fee.

This guide explains the three inverter families you will be offered in India - string, micro, and hybrid - with realistic 2026 costs and a simple way to choose.

## What a Solar Inverter Actually Does

- **Converts DC to AC** so your appliances and the grid can use solar power.
- **Maximises output** using MPPT (Maximum Power Point Tracking) to squeeze the most from your panels as sunlight varies.
- **Manages grid interaction** - exporting surplus to the grid under net metering and importing when solar is short.
- **Protects the system** - shutting down safely on grid faults or, for hybrids, switching to battery/backup.

Inverter capacity is sized to your array. A 3kW system usually pairs with a ~3kW (or slightly larger) inverter. Getting this ratio right protects both generation and warranty.

## String Inverters: The Indian Default

A **string inverter** connects a "string" of panels in series to one central unit, usually mounted on a wall near your meter.

- **Best for:** unshaded rooftops with panels facing the same direction - most Indian homes.
- **Cost:** lowest per watt; already built into the typical residential turnkey price of **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW**.
- **Pros:** cheapest, simplest, easiest to service, proven, widely stocked.
- **Cons:** if one panel is shaded or dirty, it can drag down the whole string's output; single point of failure.

For a standard, sun-facing terrace, a string inverter is the sensible, cost-effective choice and what most quotes assume by default.

## Microinverters: One Per Panel

**Microinverters** sit under each panel and convert DC to AC right there, so every panel works independently.

- **Best for:** roofs with partial shading (water tanks, parapets, trees), multiple orientations, or complex layouts.
- **Cost:** a clear premium - often adding roughly Rs 8,000-20,000+ per kW over a string setup, depending on brand.
- **Pros:** panel-level optimisation (shade on one panel does not hurt others), panel-level monitoring, no single central failure point, safer low-voltage DC.
- **Cons:** more expensive, more units on the roof to maintain, not needed for clean unshaded roofs.

**Example:** A Bengaluru home with a large overhead tank shading two panels for part of the day may lose meaningful output on a string system. Microinverters (or a string inverter with power optimisers) let the unshaded panels run at full output. If the shading loss is significant, the ~Rs 15,000/kW premium can pay for itself; if shading is minor, it usually does not.

## Hybrid Inverters: Solar Plus Battery-Ready

A **hybrid inverter** is a string-type inverter that can also charge and draw from a battery and provide backup during power cuts.

- **Best for:** areas with frequent outages, or homeowners who want battery backup now or later.
- **Cost:** more than a plain on-grid string inverter; the bigger cost is the battery itself.
- **Pros:** runs essential loads during outages, stores surplus solar for evenings, future-proofs for a battery even if you add it later.
- **Cons:** costs more upfront; a plain on-grid system that runs on net metering is cheaper if you do not need backup.

Note: a standard on-grid (grid-tied) system - hybrid or string - **shuts off during a grid outage for safety** unless it has a battery and backup capability. If uninterrupted power during cuts matters to you, a hybrid with battery is the path.

## Efficiency, Monitoring and Warranty

- **Efficiency:** modern inverters convert at ~97-98%. Differences between good brands are small; installation quality matters more.
- **Monitoring:** most inverters offer app-based monitoring so you can see daily units. Microinverters add per-panel visibility, useful for spotting a failing panel.
- **Warranty:** inverters typically carry a shorter warranty than panels - often 5-10 years (extendable), versus the ~25-year performance warranty on modules. Because the inverter is the most likely component to need replacement over 25 years, warranty and local service support are worth prioritising.

## How to Choose: A Quick Decision Guide

- **Clean, single-orientation, unshaded roof, no backup needed:** string inverter (default, lowest cost).
- **Partial shading or mixed orientations:** microinverters or power optimisers.
- **Frequent power cuts / want backup:** hybrid inverter (add battery now or later).
- **Planning to expand later:** size the inverter with a little headroom and ask about oversizing limits.

Match the inverter to your roof and your electricity reality, not to a salesperson's upsell. On a simple sunny terrace with a reliable grid and net metering, the string inverter usually wins on value - and keeps your residential payback in the typical 3-5 year range.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Which inverter type is best for most Indian homes?**
A string inverter, for most clean, unshaded, single-orientation rooftops. It is the lowest-cost option and already included in standard turnkey pricing. Only move to micro or hybrid if shading or backup needs justify the premium.

**Q: Do microinverters really generate more electricity?**
They mainly help when panels are shaded or face different directions, by stopping one weak panel from dragging down the rest. On a fully sunlit, uniform roof the extra generation is small and often not worth the added cost.

**Q: Will my solar work during a power cut?**
Only if you have a hybrid inverter with a battery and backup capability. Standard on-grid systems shut down during outages for grid-safety reasons, even though the sun is shining.

**Q: How long do solar inverters last?**
Typically 10-15 years, with warranties often 5-10 years (sometimes extendable). Expect to replace the inverter at least once over the panels' 25-year life, so factor a future inverter cost into your planning.

**Q: Does the inverter type affect my subsidy?**
The PM Surya Ghar subsidy is based on system capacity (Rs 30,000/kW up to 2kW, then Rs 18,000/kW to 3kW, capped at Rs 78,000), not inverter type - but your system and vendor must be MNRE-empanelled with ALMM panels. Any compliant inverter type is fine.

**Q: Can I add a battery later to a system installed today?**
Easily if you install a hybrid (battery-ready) inverter now. With a plain on-grid string inverter, adding a battery later may require additional equipment or an inverter change, so decide upfront if backup is likely.
