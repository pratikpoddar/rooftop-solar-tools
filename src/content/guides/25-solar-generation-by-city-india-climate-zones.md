How much electricity your rooftop solar system produces depends heavily on where you live. India spans arid deserts, humid coasts, high mountains, and rain-heavy zones, and each affects sunlight hours and panel output. This guide gives realistic generation figures by city and climate zone so you can set accurate expectations before you buy.

## The National Baseline: About 4 Units per kW per Day

Across most of India, a well-installed rooftop system generates roughly **4 units (kWh) per kW per day**, averaged over the year. That works out to:

- **~120 units per month per kW**
- **~1,400-1,500 units per year per kW**
- A **3 kW system:** roughly **330-420 units/month**

This is an annual average. Real output swings with season, with the strongest generation in the dry, clear months (October to March in much of the country) and the weakest during the monsoon.

## Why Generation Varies by City

Several local factors move your output above or below the 4-units baseline:

- **Solar irradiance:** How much sunlight energy hits your roof, higher in the north-west and Deccan plateau.
- **Cloud cover and monsoon intensity:** Coastal and eastern cities lose more output in the rainy months.
- **Ambient temperature:** Extreme heat slightly reduces panel efficiency, so very hot cities may not out-generate moderate-temperature high-irradiance zones.
- **Dust and pollution:** Reduce output if panels are not cleaned regularly.
- **Shading and orientation:** Local, but decisive.

## Generation by Climate Zone

### High-Irradiance / Arid Zone (Rajasthan, Gujarat, parts of MP)
Cities like **Jaipur, Jodhpur, Ahmedabad, and Bhuj** enjoy long, clear, sunny spells and are among India's best solar geographies. Expect the upper end of the range, often **4.2-4.7 units/kW/day** annually. A 3 kW system can push toward 400+ units/month in good months.

### Deccan Plateau / Semi-Arid (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune)
Moderate temperatures and generally good sun. **Bengaluru (BESCOM), Hyderabad, and Pune** typically see **~4.0-4.3 units/kW/day**. Bengaluru's mild climate helps panel efficiency, though it has a distinct cloudy monsoon dip.

### Northern Plains (Delhi NCR, Lucknow, Chandigarh)
Strong summer and post-monsoon generation, but winter fog and high pollution can trim output. **Delhi NCR** averages around **~4.0 units/kW/day**, with a noticeable dip during December-January fog.

### Humid Coastal Zone (Mumbai, Chennai, Kochi)
Good annual sun but heavier monsoon cloud cover. **Mumbai** and **Chennai (TANGEDCO)** typically deliver **~3.8-4.2 units/kW/day** on an annual average, with a sharp monsoon reduction offset by strong dry-season output.

### High-Rainfall / Eastern Zone (Kolkata, Guwahati, coastal Kerala)
Extended monsoons and cloudy stretches place these on the lower side, often **~3.6-4.0 units/kW/day** annually. Systems still pay back well, but plan for a longer wet-season lull.

## Seasonal Swings You Should Expect

Even in a high-sun city, monthly output is not flat:

- **Peak (Oct-Mar):** Clear skies and moderate temperatures can lift output 15-25% above the annual average.
- **Summer (Apr-Jun):** Long days help, but extreme heat slightly lowers panel efficiency.
- **Monsoon (Jul-Sep):** Cloud cover can cut daily generation by 30-50% for weeks. This is normal, not a fault.

Net metering smooths these swings by banking your dry-season surplus against monsoon shortfalls, so your **annual** bill saving stays strong even if individual months dip.

## A Worked Savings Example

Consider a **3 kW system in Pune** generating about 380 units/month on average, roughly 4,560 units/year. If your effective tariff is around Rs 9-10 per unit including taxes, that is roughly **Rs 41,000-45,000 saved per year**. At a pre-subsidy cost of Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh minus the Rs 78,000 central subsidy, your net outlay is around Rs 87,000-1.32 lakh, giving a payback of roughly **3-4 years**, well within the typical residential range.

## How to Protect Your Generation

- **Clean panels regularly**, especially before and after monsoon and in dusty cities.
- **Avoid shading** from new construction, tanks, or growing trees.
- **Prefer south-facing, correctly tilted layouts.**
- **Monitor output** via your inverter app and flag sustained underperformance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many units does 1 kW of solar generate in India?
About 4 units per day on an annual average, or roughly 120 units per month and 1,400-1,500 units per year, varying by city and season.

### Which Indian cities are best for solar?
Arid, high-irradiance regions like Jaipur, Jodhpur, and Ahmedabad top the list, followed by plateau cities such as Bengaluru, Hyderabad, and Pune.

### Does solar work during the monsoon?
Yes, but output can fall 30-50% during heavy cloud cover. Net metering banks your dry-season surplus, keeping your annual savings intact.

### Does extreme heat increase solar generation?
Not directly. More sunlight helps, but very high panel temperatures slightly reduce efficiency, which is why some hot cities do not out-generate cooler high-irradiance zones.

### Will my city's numbers match this guide exactly?
Treat these as annual averages. Your real output depends on shading, orientation, tilt, cleaning, and equipment, so use your installer's site-specific estimate for planning.
