Every year, state electricity regulators approve new tariff orders, and almost every year the number goes up. If you have watched your bill climb even though your usage stayed the same, you have already felt it. Rooftop solar is one of the few ways an ordinary household or business can step off that escalator. This article explains why tariffs rise, how much, and why solar acts as a long-term hedge.

## Why electricity tariffs keep rising in India

Grid electricity gets more expensive for structural reasons that are not going away:

- **Fuel costs** - coal and gas prices feed directly into generation costs.
- **DISCOM finances** - many distribution companies run losses and periodically seek tariff increases to recover them.
- **Network and capacity investment** - upgrading transmission, distribution, and adding capacity costs money that ends up in tariffs.
- **Cross-subsidy and duties** - residential and commercial slabs, plus fixed charges, fuel surcharges, and electricity duty, all tend to drift upward.

Across India, retail tariffs have risen roughly **3-5% per year** over the long run, and commercial and industrial tariffs are usually higher than residential to begin with.

## What a 3-5% annual hike does over time

A small yearly increase compounds into a large one. Consider a household paying Rs 5,000 a month today.

- At **4% escalation**, that Rs 5,000 bill becomes about Rs 7,400 in 10 years and about Rs 10,900 in 20 years.
- Over 25 years, you would pay lakhs more for the exact same electricity, simply because the price per unit kept rising.

The unit consumption never changed. Only the tariff did. That is the cost solar helps you avoid.

## How solar works as a hedge

A hedge is anything that protects you from a price you cannot control. Rooftop solar does this in a direct way:

- **It converts a variable cost into a fixed one.** You pay once (largely upfront), and your cost per unit generated is effectively locked for 25 years.
- **Its value rises with every hike.** Each unit your panels produce offsets a grid unit that keeps getting more expensive. So while your generation stays roughly flat, the rupee savings grow year after year.
- **It shrinks your exposure.** If solar covers 70-90% of your consumption, only the small remainder is exposed to future tariff increases.

In other words, the worse grid tariffs get, the better your solar investment looks.

### A worked example

Take a 3kW system generating about 360 units a month, offsetting power at a starting tariff of Rs 8/unit.

- **Year 1 savings:** roughly 4,320 units x Rs 8 = about Rs 34,500.
- With **4% tariff escalation**, the same generation is worth about Rs 41,900 by year 5 and about Rs 51,000 by year 10, because the grid price it replaces has climbed.
- Meanwhile the system cost was fixed at roughly Rs 90,000-1.3 lakh net of the Rs 78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy.

Your savings grow precisely because tariffs rise, which is the essence of a hedge.

## Fixed cost of solar power vs the rising grid

The all-in cost of a unit from your own rooftop is set the day you install. Spread a net cost of, say, Rs 1.1 lakh for a 3kW system across the roughly 1,00,000+ units it will generate over 25 years, and your effective cost per unit is very low, often a small fraction of today's grid tariff, and it does not rise.

Grid power, by contrast, starts higher and climbs every year. Over the life of the system, the gap between your fixed solar cost and the ever-rising grid rate widens continuously.

## Who benefits most from the hedge

- **High-slab residential users** - the more you consume in the expensive top slabs, the more each hike hurts and the more solar protects you.
- **SMEs and commercial users** - commercial tariffs are higher and often rise faster, so shops, clinics, and small offices see an even stronger hedging benefit. Businesses can also claim accelerated depreciation of about 40% on solar assets.
- **Anyone planning to stay put for years** - the longer your horizon, the more compounding tariff hikes you avoid.

## Limits and honest caveats

Solar is a strong hedge, not a magic wall:

- **You still pay for the grid at night** unless you add a battery, so a small portion of your bill remains exposed to hikes.
- **Fixed charges and net-metering fees** (a one-time Rs 3,000-8,000, up to about Rs 15,000 for three-phase) are unavoidable.
- **Export rates can change** - if a state revises net-metering credits downward, surplus export becomes less valuable, so sizing to your own consumption is the safest hedge.
- **Policy can evolve** - subsidies and net-metering rules are set by MNRE and states and can be revised, so act while current terms are favourable.

Even with these caveats, locking in the price of most of your electricity for 25 years is a rare and valuable protection.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much do electricity tariffs rise each year in India?**
Retail tariffs have historically risen roughly 3-5% per year on average, with commercial and industrial tariffs typically higher and sometimes increasing faster than residential.

**How does solar protect me from tariff hikes?**
Solar fixes your cost of self-generated power for about 25 years while the grid price it replaces keeps rising. So your rupee savings grow with every hike, even though your generation stays flat.

**Will solar fully insulate me from electricity price increases?**
Not entirely. Night-time use, fixed charges, and any consumption beyond what your system covers remain exposed. But a well-sized system can eliminate 70-90% of your exposure.

**Does the hedge work better for businesses?**
Yes. Commercial tariffs are higher and often rise faster, and businesses can add tax benefits like roughly 40% accelerated depreciation, making the hedge financially stronger for SMEs.

**Could future policy changes weaken solar's benefit?**
Subsidies and net-metering rules can change over time. Installing while the PM Surya Ghar subsidy and current net-metering terms apply locks in favourable economics, and your generation savings continue regardless.
