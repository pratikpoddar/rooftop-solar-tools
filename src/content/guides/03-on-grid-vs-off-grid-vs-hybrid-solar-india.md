# On-Grid vs Off-Grid vs Hybrid Solar Systems: Which Is Right for Your Indian Home?

**Meta Description:** Compare on-grid, off-grid and hybrid solar systems for Indian homes in 2026: costs in Rs, backup, subsidy eligibility and net metering, to pick the right setup.
**Target Keyword:** on grid vs off grid solar India
**Secondary Keywords:** hybrid solar system India, solar battery backup, net metering, PM Surya Ghar eligibility, off grid solar cost
**Search Intent:** Comparison

When you plan a rooftop solar system in India, one of the first choices is the type: on-grid, off-grid, or hybrid. They look similar on the roof, but they differ sharply in cost, backup ability and subsidy eligibility. This guide compares all three so you can pick the right one for your home.

## The three system types at a glance

- **On-grid (grid-tied):** Connected to your DISCOM's grid, no batteries. Exports surplus and uses net metering. Cheapest and subsidy-eligible.
- **Off-grid (standalone):** No grid connection at all. Relies entirely on batteries for storage. Used where the grid is absent or unreliable.
- **Hybrid:** Grid-connected but with a battery, so you get net metering plus backup during power cuts.

## On-grid solar: the default choice for most homes

On-grid is what the vast majority of Indian homeowners install, and it is the setup the PM Surya Ghar subsidy is designed for.

**How it works:** Panels feed the inverter, which powers your home. Surplus flows to the grid through a net meter; you draw from the grid at night. No battery is involved.

**Pros:**
- Lowest cost, roughly Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW turnkey.
- Eligible for the PM Surya Ghar central subsidy (up to Rs 78,000 for 3kW and above).
- Simple, low-maintenance, long panel life (~25 years).
- Net metering credits reduce your bill directly.

**Cons:**
- No backup during a grid outage; the system shuts off for safety when the grid is down.

**Best for:** Homes in areas with a stable grid and only occasional, short power cuts, who want the fastest payback (typically 3-5 years).

## Off-grid solar: independence, at a price

Off-grid systems are fully self-contained. They generate, store in batteries, and supply power with no grid connection.

**Pros:**
- Complete energy independence; works where there is no grid at all.
- Power even during long outages, as everything runs off the battery bank.

**Cons:**
- Much more expensive because of large battery banks that need replacing every few years.
- Not eligible for net metering, and generally outside the PM Surya Ghar subsidy framework.
- You must size the battery for the worst-case cloudy stretch, or risk running out.

**Best for:** Remote homes, farmhouses, or locations without reliable grid access.

## Hybrid solar: net metering plus backup

A hybrid system is the best of both worlds, and the most expensive of the everyday options. It stays connected to the grid, uses net metering, and adds a battery so essential loads keep running during a cut.

**Pros:**
- Backup power during outages for chosen circuits (lights, fans, fridge, Wi-Fi).
- Still exports surplus and earns net-metering credits.
- Flexible: can prioritise battery charging or grid export.

**Cons:**
- Battery adds substantial cost and is not subsidised.
- Longer payback than a plain on-grid system.
- Batteries need periodic replacement.

**Best for:** Homes in areas with frequent or long power cuts who want both savings and reliability.

## Cost and subsidy comparison

Here is how the three compare for a typical home:

- **On-grid, 3kW:** ~Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh pre-subsidy, minus Rs 78,000 subsidy. Best value.
- **Hybrid, 3kW:** On-grid cost plus battery, often adding Rs 60,000-1.5 lakh depending on battery size; only the base system may attract subsidy where allowed.
- **Off-grid, 3kW:** Highest total cost due to large batteries; no net metering, typically no subsidy.

Remember to also budget Rs 3,000-8,000 (up to ~Rs 15,000 for three-phase) for DISCOM net-metering charges on grid-connected systems.

## A quick decision framework

Ask yourself:

1. **How reliable is my grid?** Stable grid, short cuts, choose on-grid. Frequent long cuts, consider hybrid. No grid, off-grid.
2. **Do I want the subsidy?** On-grid maximises subsidy value; off-grid usually forfeits it.
3. **What is my budget and payback goal?** On-grid pays back fastest (3-5 years); batteries lengthen that.
4. **How much backup do I really need?** Sizing a hybrid battery for just essential loads keeps cost sensible.

## A real example: choosing for a Chennai home

A Chennai family with a Rs 4,000 bill and occasional monsoon-season outages compared options for a 3kW system:

- **On-grid:** ~Rs 1.9 lakh pre-subsidy, Rs 78,000 subsidy, net ~Rs 1.12 lakh, payback ~3-4 years, but no outage backup.
- **Hybrid with a small battery:** Roughly Rs 80,000 more for backup on essential circuits, stretching payback by a couple of years.

They chose on-grid for the fast payback and added a small inverter-battery only for the fridge and lights, a common, cost-effective compromise.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which solar system is eligible for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?**
On-grid residential systems using ALMM-listed panels and an MNRE-empanelled vendor are the standard subsidy-eligible choice. Off-grid systems generally do not qualify.

**Does an on-grid system work during a power cut?**
No. For safety, a grid-tied system switches off when the grid goes down. If you need backup, choose a hybrid system.

**Is a hybrid system worth the extra cost?**
It is worth it where power cuts are frequent or long. Where the grid is stable, the battery premium usually is not justified, and on-grid gives a faster payback.

**Do off-grid systems get net metering?**
No. Net metering requires a grid connection. Off-grid systems store surplus in batteries instead of exporting it.

**How much backup does a hybrid battery give?**
It depends on battery size and which loads you connect. Many homes size the battery only for essentials such as lights, fans, fridge and router to keep costs down.

**Can I add a battery later to an on-grid system?**
Sometimes, if the inverter is hybrid-ready. If not, you may need to replace the inverter, so decide early if backup matters to you.
