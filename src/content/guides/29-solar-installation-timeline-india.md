# Solar Installation Timeline in India: From Quote to Switch-On

Most Indian homeowners are surprised by one thing about rooftop solar: the panels go up in a day or two, but the whole journey from first quote to a working, grid-connected system usually takes four to eight weeks. The physical work is fast. The paperwork, DISCOM approvals, and net-meter installation are what set the pace.

This guide breaks down each stage so you know what to expect, where delays happen, and how to keep your project moving.

## The Big Picture: 4 to 8 Weeks

For a standard residential system (say 3 kW) applying through the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana portal, a realistic end-to-end timeline is:

- **Quotes and vendor selection:** 3-10 days
- **Portal application and DISCOM feasibility approval:** 5-15 days
- **Installation on your roof:** 1-3 days
- **Net meter application, inspection, and commissioning:** 1-3 weeks
- **Subsidy credit to your bank account:** 30-45 days after commissioning

Add it up and most homeowners are generating power within a month, with the government subsidy landing a few weeks later. Larger or three-phase systems, or projects in busy DISCOM circles, sit at the longer end.

## Stage 1: Getting Quotes and Choosing a Vendor (3-10 Days)

Start by collecting two or three quotes from MNRE-empanelled vendors. Ask each for a written quotation covering system size, panel make (ALMM-listed mono PERC is the 2026 standard), inverter brand, structure, cabling, and the total price.

At around Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW for a turnkey residential system in 2026, a 3 kW system costs roughly Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh before subsidy. Comparing quotes carefully here prevents expensive surprises later.

**Tip:** Do not sign the first quote you receive. Read our checklist on [What to Check in a Solar Quotation Before You Sign in India](./30-what-to-check-solar-quotation-india).

## Stage 2: Portal Application and DISCOM Feasibility (5-15 Days)

Register on pmsuryaghar.gov.in, submit your application with your electricity bill and consumer details, and select your vendor. Your DISCOM then runs a **feasibility check** to confirm your connection can support the proposed system.

This is the first common bottleneck. Approval can take anywhere from a few days to two weeks depending on how busy your local DISCOM circle is. You cannot legally begin installation until feasibility approval comes through.

**What can slow it down:**

- Incomplete documents or a name mismatch between the electricity bill and applicant
- Sanctioned load lower than your proposed system size
- Pending dues on the electricity account

## Stage 3: Installation on Your Roof (1-3 Days)

Once approved, the actual installation is quick. For a typical home:

- **Day 1:** Mounting structure fixed, panels placed, DC cabling run
- **Day 2:** Inverter mounted, AC wiring, earthing, and lightning protection completed
- **Day 3 (if needed):** Testing, tidy-up, and system commissioning checks

A 3 kW system needs only about 200-300 sq ft of shadow-free roof, so most homes are covered comfortably. Rooftop safety, waterproofing around mounts, and neat cable management are the things to watch during this stage.

## Stage 4: Net Metering, Inspection, and Commissioning (1-3 Weeks)

After installation, your vendor applies for the **net meter** (or you may pay a DISCOM net-metering charge of roughly Rs 3,000-8,000, and up to about Rs 15,000 for three-phase connections). A DISCOM official then inspects the installation and installs a bidirectional meter that records both the power you draw and the surplus you export.

The system is officially **commissioned** once this meter is installed and the inspection passes. From this date, your net-metering credits begin and your subsidy clock starts.

This stage is the second common delay because it depends entirely on DISCOM scheduling. Following up politely with your vendor and DISCOM keeps things moving.

## Stage 5: Subsidy Disbursement (30-45 Days)

Under PM Surya Ghar, the central subsidy is credited directly to your bank account (DBT) roughly **30 to 45 days after commissioning and inspection**. The amounts in 2026 are:

- Rs 30,000 for 1 kW
- Rs 60,000 for 2 kW
- Rs 78,000 for 3 kW and above (capped)

The slab works out to Rs 30,000/kW for the first 2 kW, Rs 18,000/kW for the 2-3 kW band, and nil above 3 kW. Some states add their own top-up on the portal, which can arrive on a separate timeline.

## A Worked Example: 3 kW Home in Bengaluru

- **Week 1:** Collected three quotes, chose an MNRE-empanelled vendor at Rs 1.9 lakh
- **Week 2:** Applied on the portal; DISCOM feasibility approved on day 9
- **Week 3:** Panels installed over two days; net meter applied for
- **Week 4-5:** DISCOM inspection done; bidirectional meter installed; system commissioned
- **Week 9-10:** Rs 78,000 subsidy credited to bank account

The homeowner started generating around 12 units a day (a 3 kW system produces roughly 330-420 units a month) from the commissioning date, well before the subsidy arrived.

## How to Avoid Delays

- **Keep documents ready:** electricity bill, ID, address proof, bank details, and a clear roof photo.
- **Fix account issues first:** clear pending dues and correct any name mismatch on the bill.
- **Choose an experienced vendor:** they know your DISCOM's process and follow up on approvals for you.
- **Respond fast:** approve quotes, sign, and share documents promptly so the vendor is never waiting on you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does rooftop solar installation take in India?**
From first quote to switch-on, expect four to eight weeks. The rooftop work itself is only one to three days; the rest is DISCOM feasibility approval, net metering, and inspection.

**Can I use my solar system before net metering is installed?**
No. The system is commissioned only after the DISCOM installs the bidirectional net meter and inspects the installation. Using it before then is not permitted and means no export credits.

**When will I receive the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?**
The central subsidy is transferred to your bank account by DBT roughly 30-45 days after commissioning and inspection. In 2026 it is Rs 78,000 for a 3 kW or larger system.

**What causes the longest delays?**
DISCOM feasibility approval and net-meter installation are the two slowest stages, since both depend on DISCOM scheduling rather than your vendor.

**Does a bigger or three-phase system take longer?**
Often yes. Larger and three-phase connections may need extra approvals and a higher net-metering charge (up to about Rs 15,000), which can extend the timeline by a week or two.

**Do I need to be present during installation?**
It helps to be available on the first day for roof access and final placement decisions, but you do not need to supervise the entire installation.
