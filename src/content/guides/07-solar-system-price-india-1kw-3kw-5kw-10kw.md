## How solar is priced in India

Rooftop solar is priced per kilowatt (kW) of capacity. In 2026, residential systems cost roughly **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW** turnkey (about Rs 40-55 per watt), covering panels, inverter, structure, wiring, installation and paperwork. Larger systems have a slightly lower per-kW cost. Commercial-scale systems run cheaper, around Rs 38-50 per watt.

On top of hardware, budget Rs 3,000-8,000 for net-metering and DISCOM charges (up to about Rs 15,000 for three-phase connections).

## The subsidy that applies to home systems

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana central subsidy for residential systems is:

- **1kW:** Rs 30,000
- **2kW:** Rs 60,000
- **3kW and above:** Rs 78,000 (capped)

It is calculated at Rs 30,000 per kW for the first 2kW and Rs 18,000 per kW for the third kW, with nothing above 3kW. Crucially, the subsidy is **capped at Rs 78,000** no matter how large the system, so 5kW and 10kW systems still receive only Rs 78,000. Some states add a top-up. The amount is paid by Direct Benefit Transfer, usually 30-45 days after commissioning.

## Why 3kW is the value sweet spot

Because the subsidy is capped at Rs 78,000 for 3kW and above, a 3kW system captures the maximum benefit relative to its price. Every kW beyond 3kW is paid at full cost with no extra subsidy. If your usage justifies a larger system, it still pays back, but the subsidy leverage is strongest at 3kW.

## Generation and payback at a glance

Using the rule of about 4 units per day per kW:

- **3kW:** ~330-420 units/month, payback ~3-5 years
- **5kW:** ~550-700 units/month, payback typically ~4-5 years
- **10kW:** ~1,100-1,400 units/month, payback depends on how much you self-consume vs export

Residential payback generally lands in the 3-5 year range, after which power is effectively free for the rest of the panels' ~25-year life.

## A worked example: comparing 3kW and 5kW in Mumbai

A family deciding between two sizes with a Rs 6,000 monthly bill:

- **3kW:** ~Rs 1.9 lakh pre-subsidy, Rs 78,000 subsidy, net ~Rs 1.12 lakh, ~330-420 units/month. Covers most of the bill; fastest payback.
- **5kW:** ~Rs 3 lakh pre-subsidy, Rs 78,000 subsidy, net ~Rs 2.22 lakh, ~550-700 units/month. Covers the full bill and builds export credits, but the extra 2kW is unsubsidised.

They chose 5kW because their heavy AC usage justified the larger array, accepting a slightly longer payback for full bill coverage.

## Tips to get an accurate price

- Share 6-12 months of bills so the vendor sizes the system to your real usage.
- Get itemised, all-inclusive quotes from MNRE-empanelled vendors.
- Confirm panels are ALMM-listed and BIS-compliant.
- Check whether your connection is single or three-phase, as it affects net-metering charges.
- Factor in state top-ups, which can further reduce net cost.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the price of a 3kW solar system in India in 2026?**
About Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh before subsidy, and roughly Rs 90,000-1.3 lakh after the Rs 78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy, plus small net-metering charges.

**How much does a 5kW solar system cost?**
Around Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh pre-subsidy. The subsidy is capped at Rs 78,000, so net cost is roughly Rs 1.7-2.7 lakh.

**What does a 10kW system cost?**
Roughly Rs 4.5-6.5 lakh before subsidy. With the Rs 78,000 cap applied, net cost is about Rs 3.7-5.7 lakh.

**Does a bigger system get a bigger subsidy?**
No. The central subsidy is capped at Rs 78,000 for any system of 3kW or more, so larger systems do not receive a proportionally larger subsidy.

**How much power will each size generate?**
Plan for about 4 units per day per kW: roughly 120 units/month for 1kW, 330-420 for 3kW, 550-700 for 5kW and 1,100-1,400 for 10kW.

**Which size gives the best value?**
3kW offers the best subsidy leverage because it captures the full Rs 78,000. Larger systems still pay back if your consumption justifies them.
