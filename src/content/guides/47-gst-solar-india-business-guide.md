If your business is buying rooftop solar, GST is one of the numbers that decides your real cost. The headline is simple: **GST on solar modules and systems is currently 12%.** But how that 12% flows through your books - as input tax credit, and alongside depreciation - determines whether it is a genuine cost or largely recoverable. This guide breaks it down for Indian businesses in 2026.

## The Headline: 12% GST on Solar

Solar photovoltaic modules and solar power generating systems attract **GST at 12%** in India. This applies to the equipment and, in most turnkey supplies, the overall system. It is a meaningful line item: on a Rs 12 lakh, 25 kW commercial system, 12% GST is roughly Rs 1.28 lakh (on the taxable value).

The key question for a business is not whether you pay GST - you do - but whether you can **recover it as input tax credit**.

## Input Tax Credit: The Business Advantage

For a GST-registered business, the GST paid on a solar system is generally available as **input tax credit (ITC)**, which you set off against your output GST liability. In effect, a large part of the 12% comes back to you rather than being a sunk cost.

To claim ITC cleanly:

- Buy from a **GST-registered installer** who issues a proper **tax invoice** showing your **GSTIN**.
- Ensure the invoice correctly describes the goods/services and the GST charged.
- Confirm the asset is used in the course of your business.

Because ITC availability depends on your business activity and how the system is used, always confirm eligibility with your Chartered Accountant. For most eligible SMEs, the **net cost after ITC is lower than the sticker price**.

## Homeowners vs Businesses

The GST treatment is the same 12% for everyone, but the ability to recover it is not:

- **Businesses** (GST-registered) can typically claim ITC, so the effective GST cost is reduced.
- **Homeowners** cannot claim ITC - GST is simply part of their system cost. Instead, homeowners are supported by the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy (Rs 30,000/kW for the first 2 kW, Rs 18,000/kW for 2-3 kW, capped at Rs 78,000), which is **residential only and not available to businesses**.

So businesses recover value through ITC and depreciation; homes recover value through the residential subsidy.

## How GST Fits With Your Overall Solar Economics

For a business, GST is one of three financial levers that lower the real cost of going solar:

1. **GST input credit (12%)** reduces the effective purchase price.
2. **Accelerated depreciation (~40% p.a., plus additional depreciation where applicable)** reduces taxable profit in the early years.
3. **Higher commercial tariffs (Rs 8-12/unit)** mean fast energy payback, typically 3-5 years.

### Worked Example: 25 kW System

- Base system cost (taxable value): ~Rs 12 lakh
- GST at 12%: ~Rs 1.28 lakh, largely recoverable as ITC for an eligible business
- First-year depreciation (~40%): ~Rs 4.8 lakh, saving ~Rs 1.44 lakh in tax at 30%
- Energy saving: ~Rs 3.6 lakh/year (3,000 units/month at Rs 10/unit)

Between recoverable GST, depreciation and energy savings, the effective payback often lands well inside the 3-5 year band, after which power is near-free for the balance of the ~25-year system life.

## Practical Invoicing And Compliance Tips

- **Always get a GST tax invoice** with your GSTIN - a quotation or informal bill is not enough to claim ITC.
- **Keep commissioning and capitalisation records**, since these matter for depreciation timing as well.
- **Match the invoice to ALMM-listed, BIS-compliant equipment** - this protects both your warranty and your claim.
- **Reconcile the ITC** in your GST returns and retain supplier records.
- **Under OPEX/RESCO**, you don't buy the system, so there's no ITC on the asset for you - you pay for power instead, and the developer handles GST on the equipment. Choose CAPEX if capturing ITC and depreciation yourself is a priority.

## Common Misconceptions

- **"Solar is GST-free."** Not for businesses buying systems - the rate is 12%.
- **"GST makes solar too expensive."** For GST-registered businesses, most of it is recoverable as ITC, so the net impact is limited.
- **"I can claim the residential subsidy and GST credit together."** No - the PM Surya Ghar subsidy is for homes, and ITC is for businesses; they don't stack on the same installation.

Rates and rules can change with each Budget or GST Council decision, so verify the current position with your CA before you finalise a purchase.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the GST rate on solar in India?**
GST on solar modules and systems is currently 12%.

**Can my business claim input tax credit on solar?**
Yes, in most cases. A GST-registered business can typically claim the 12% GST as input tax credit, provided the installer issues a proper tax invoice with your GSTIN and the asset is used for business.

**Does GST apply to homeowners too?**
Yes, the 12% rate is the same, but homeowners cannot claim ITC. They instead benefit from the residential PM Surya Ghar subsidy.

**How much is GST on a 25 kW commercial system?**
About Rs 1.28 lakh on a ~Rs 12 lakh system - but for an eligible business, most of it is recoverable as input tax credit.

**Do I get ITC under an OPEX/RESCO model?**
No. Under OPEX/RESCO you don't own the asset, so there's no ITC on the equipment for you; you pay for power at an agreed tariff instead.

**What documents do I need to claim GST credit?**
A GST tax invoice showing your GSTIN, correct description of goods/services, and records that the asset is used in your business.
