Before you worry about panel brands or subsidies, one physical constraint decides everything: how much usable, shadow-free roof you actually have. This guide gives you clear thumb rules, appliance-free calculations, and worked examples so you can estimate your maximum system size in minutes.

## The Basic Thumb Rule: About 100 sq ft per kW

For a standard rooftop solar installation in India, plan for roughly:

- **80-100 sq ft (about 7-10 sq m) of shadow-free roof per 1 kW.**

This includes the tilt, spacing between rows, and walking access for maintenance, not just the raw panel footprint. A single mono PERC panel of ~550 Wp measures roughly 2.3 m x 1.1 m (about 27 sq ft), and a 1 kW system uses about 2 such panels. The extra area beyond the bare panels covers the mounting structure angle and gaps.

|---|---|---|---|

Because the PM Surya Ghar central subsidy caps at **Rs 78,000 for 3 kW and above**, many homeowners target 3 kW, which needs roughly 250-320 sq ft, an area most independent houses can provide.

## Why You Need More Than the Panel Footprint

Several real-world factors mean your usable roof is smaller than your total roof:

- **Tilt and row spacing:** Panels are tilted (often 10-30 degrees facing south) and rows must be spaced so front rows do not shade the ones behind.
- **Maintenance walkways:** Cleaning and inspection need clear access paths.
- **Obstructions:** Water tanks, staircases (mumty), parapets, dish antennas, vents, and chimneys eat into space.
- **Setbacks:** Panels are usually kept slightly away from the roof edge for safety and wind load.

A practical rule: assume only **60-75% of your total terrace is usable** for panels after obstructions.

## Shading Is the Silent Killer

Roof area is useless if it is shaded. Solar panels perform best in direct, unobstructed sunlight, and shade on even part of a panel can drag down output disproportionately. Watch for:

- Overhead water tanks and their shadows moving through the day.
- Neighbouring taller buildings, especially in dense urban plots.
- Trees that grow over time.
- Nearby mobile towers or hoardings.

The best-performing area is a **south-facing, shadow-free** stretch that stays clear from roughly 9 am to 4 pm, when generation peaks. Installers use a shadow analysis (often over a full day) to map this before finalising layout.

## Worked Examples for Indian Homes

### Example 1: Independent House, 900 sq ft Terrace
A standalone house with a 900 sq ft flat terrace, minus a water tank and staircase mumty, might leave ~600 sq ft usable. That comfortably supports a **5 kW system (9-10 panels, ~450-550 sq ft)** with room to spare. Cost roughly Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh before subsidy; generation about 600 units/month.

### Example 2: Row House, 300 sq ft Usable Roof
A compact urban home with ~300 sq ft of clear roof fits a **3 kW system (250-320 sq ft)**. This maximises the central subsidy at Rs 78,000, generates roughly 330-420 units/month, and suits a family consuming 300-400 units.

### Example 3: Apartment Flat
Individual flat owners rarely have private roof rights. Options are a share of common terrace (via society approval and a shared/net-metering arrangement) or waiting for a society-wide installation. Balcony or elevated-frame setups are possible but limited and site-specific.

## Sloped Roofs, Sheet Roofs, and Elevated Structures

- **Flat RCC terrace:** Most common; panels sit on tilted galvanised structures.
- **Sloped roofs (tiles/sheets):** Panels mount parallel to the slope. A south-facing slope is ideal; north-facing slopes generate significantly less.
- **Metal sheet (SME sheds):** Common for small businesses; panels clamp directly, often allowing high capacity because sheds are large.
- **Elevated/raised structures:** For homes short on roof, panels can be raised on a frame to preserve terrace use underneath (drying clothes, seating), at extra structural cost.

## What to Measure Before Calling an Installer

1. Total terrace length x breadth in feet.
2. Space taken by tanks, mumty, and other fixtures.
3. Direction the longest clear stretch faces (ideally south).
4. Nearby shading objects and their approximate height.
5. Whether the slab can bear the added load (a structural check is standard).

Share these and a photo, and any MNRE-empanelled vendor can give you a firm maximum kW quickly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much roof area do I need for a 3 kW system?
About 250-320 sq ft of shadow-free roof. This is the popular size because it maxes out the Rs 78,000 central subsidy.

### Can I install solar on a small terrace?
Yes. Even 100 sq ft supports about 1 kW. If space is tight, use higher-wattage panels and an elevated frame to preserve usable terrace below.

### Which direction should panels face in India?
South-facing is ideal for maximum year-round generation. East or west works with a modest output reduction; north-facing is generally avoided.

### Does a water tank affect my solar layout?
Yes. Its shadow moves through the day and can shade panels. Installers map this and place panels outside the shadow path, which reduces usable area.

### Can flat owners in apartments go solar?
Usually only through a society-approved common-terrace or shared arrangement, since individual flats lack private roof rights. A society-wide project is often the most practical route.
