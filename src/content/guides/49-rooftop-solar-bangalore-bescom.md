Bangalore is a strong market for rooftop solar. The city enjoys mild, sunny weather for most of the year, electricity tariffs keep climbing, and homeowners can combine the central PM Surya Ghar subsidy with BESCOM net metering to cut bills sharply. This homeowner's guide covers 2026 costs, the subsidy, the BESCOM process, and how to get a reliable installation.

## Your DISCOM: BESCOM

In Bangalore, your distribution company is **BESCOM (Bangalore Electricity Supply Company Limited)**. BESCOM handles your net-metering application, feasibility approval, inspection and the bi-directional meter. Your rooftop solar journey runs through BESCOM alongside the national PM Surya Ghar portal, so keep your latest BESCOM bill (with your account/RR number) handy when you apply.

## What Rooftop Solar Costs In Bangalore (2026)

For homes, turnkey rooftop solar costs about **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW** in 2026. Indicative pre-subsidy prices:

- **1 kW:** Rs 60,000-80,000
- **2 kW:** Rs 1.1-1.5 lakh
- **3 kW:** Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh
- **5 kW:** Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh
- **10 kW:** Rs 4.5-6.5 lakh

Add modest net-metering and DISCOM fees of around Rs 3,000-8,000 (up to ~Rs 15,000 for a three-phase connection). Most independent Bangalore homes go for 3-5 kW depending on roof space and bill size.

## The PM Surya Ghar Subsidy

Bangalore homeowners can claim the central **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** subsidy for residential systems:

- **Rs 30,000/kW** for the first 2 kW
- **Rs 18,000/kW** for the 2-3 kW band
- **Capped at Rs 78,000** for 3 kW and above

Apply on the national portal **pmsuryaghar.gov.in**, choose an MNRE-empanelled vendor, and use ALMM-listed modules - both are mandatory to qualify. Eligible households can also get up to 300 free units per month under the scheme's framework. The subsidy is paid by DBT to your bank account, typically around 30-45 days after BESCOM inspection and commissioning. Karnataka may offer its own additions from time to time, so check for any state top-up when you apply. The subsidy is residential only.

## A Real Example: 5 kW Bangalore Home

- **System size:** 5 kW
- **Pre-subsidy cost:** ~Rs 3 lakh (mid-range)
- **PM Surya Ghar subsidy:** Rs 78,000 (capped)
- **Net cost:** ~Rs 2.22 lakh
- **Generation:** ~20 units/day (~600 units/month), based on ~4 units/day per kW
- **Savings:** For a household paying around Rs 8/unit on higher slabs, that's roughly Rs 4,800/month, or ~Rs 58,000/year

That puts payback at roughly **4 years**, within the typical 3-5 year range - after which power is near-free for the balance of the system's ~25-year life.

## BESCOM Net Metering: Step By Step

Net metering lets you export surplus daytime generation and receive credits against future consumption. The BESCOM process generally runs:

1. Register on the national portal and pick an MNRE-empanelled installer.
2. Submit the net-metering application with your sanctioned load and proposed system size.
3. Receive feasibility approval from BESCOM.
4. Install using ALMM-listed panels and a suitable inverter.
5. BESCOM inspection and installation of the bi-directional (net) meter.
6. Commissioning and net-metering activation; subsidy DBT follows.

Confirm your **sanctioned load** can support the system, especially for 5 kW and above, and whether you have a single- or three-phase connection.

## Best Practices For Bangalore

- **Watch the monsoon dip.** Bangalore's June-September monsoon reduces generation on cloudy days; size to the annual average, not peak summer, and expect output to recover strongly in the dry months.
- **Clean panels regularly.** Dust and pollen build up between rains - a rinse every few weeks keeps output up.
- **Avoid shading.** Bangalore's tree cover and neighbouring buildings can shade rooftops; do a proper shadow assessment before finalising panel placement.
- **Insist on quality equipment.** Mono PERC panels are standard, ALMM-listed and BIS-compliant, with ~25-year performance warranties and ~0.5%/year degradation.
- **Get at least three quotes** and compare on price per Wp, brand, warranty and net-metering support - not just headline price.
- **Confirm roof rights** for apartments or shared buildings before applying.

## Do You Need A Battery In Bangalore?

For most Bangalore homes on the reliable BESCOM grid, a battery is **not** necessary - net metering handles surplus and you draw from the grid at night. A battery mainly makes sense if you face frequent outages or want backup for essentials. It adds significant cost, so for pure bill savings, size for daytime self-consumption and use net metering instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which company handles solar net metering in Bangalore?**
BESCOM (Bangalore Electricity Supply Company Limited) processes your net-metering application, inspection and bi-directional meter.

**How much does a 5 kW system cost in Bangalore in 2026?**
Around Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh before subsidy, dropping to roughly Rs 2.2 lakh after the Rs 78,000 PM Surya Ghar subsidy.

**How much subsidy can I get in Bangalore?**
Up to Rs 78,000 for a 3 kW or larger residential system - Rs 30,000/kW for the first 2 kW and Rs 18,000/kW for the 2-3 kW band.

**When will I receive the subsidy?**
By DBT to your bank account, usually around 30-45 days after BESCOM inspection and commissioning.

**What is the payback period in Bangalore?**
Typically 3-5 years for homes after subsidy, thanks to good sunshine and rising tariffs.

**Will the monsoon hurt my solar savings?**
Generation dips on cloudy monsoon days but recovers in the dry months. Sizing to the annual average keeps your savings on track.
