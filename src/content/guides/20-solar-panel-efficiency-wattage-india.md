"Which panel is more efficient?" is one of the most common questions homeowners ask - and one of the most misunderstood. Efficiency and wattage are not the same thing, and neither tells you how many units you will generate on their own. This guide explains both in plain terms, and what actually matters for output on an Indian rooftop.

## Efficiency vs Wattage: What They Really Mean

- **Wattage (Wp):** the panel's rated peak power under standard test conditions - e.g. a 550Wp panel. It tells you the panel's size of output.
- **Efficiency (%):** how much of the sunlight hitting the panel is converted to electricity - e.g. 21%. It tells you how much power you get per square metre of panel.

A helpful way to think about it: **wattage is how much a panel produces; efficiency is how compactly it produces it.** Two 550Wp panels generate similar power, but the higher-efficiency one is physically smaller - important when roof space is tight.

For mono PERC panels, the Indian mainstream in 2026, cell efficiency is typically **~19-22%**, with common module wattages of 540-590Wp and climbing.

## Why Efficiency Matters Most When Space Is Tight

Efficiency's biggest practical benefit is fitting more capacity on a limited roof.

- On a **large, unshaded roof,** you can simply add more panels, so efficiency matters less - you optimise on price per watt.
- On a **small urban terrace,** higher efficiency lets you install more kW in the same area, which can be the difference between a 2kW and a 3kW system.

**Example:** A Mumbai flat with limited terrace space can fit, say, 3kW of high-efficiency mono PERC where lower-efficiency panels would top out at 2.4kW. That extra 0.6kW generates roughly 70-90 more units a month - and, crucially, it also raises the eligible subsidy (up to the Rs 78,000 cap at 3kW). Where roof area is the binding constraint, efficiency directly translates into savings.

## Wattage Doesn't Change Units-Per-kW

A key point that saves money: your generation is driven by **installed kW**, not by whether you use fewer high-wattage panels or more lower-wattage ones.

As a planning rule, expect roughly **4 units per day per kW** in India. So:

- **1kW** -> ~120 units/month
- **3kW** -> ~330-420 units/month
- **5kW** -> ~550-700 units/month

Whether a 3kW array uses six 500Wp panels or five 600Wp panels, the generation is broadly the same. Do not pay a premium for higher wattage expecting more units per kW - pay for it only when it helps you fit more kW in limited space.

## How Indian Conditions Affect Real Output

Datasheet numbers are measured at 25 degrees C. Indian rooftops rarely stay that cool, so real output differs.

### Heat and the Temperature Coefficient
Panels lose efficiency as they heat up. The **temperature coefficient** (e.g. -0.34%/degree C) tells you how much output drops per degree above 25 degrees C. In Indian summers, panel temperatures can be 20-30 degrees C above ambient, so a better (smaller) temperature coefficient meaningfully protects output. This is one reason mono PERC is preferred over older poly panels here.

### Dust and Soiling
Dust, bird droppings and pollution reduce output regardless of rated efficiency. Soiling losses of 5-15% are common without cleaning. No panel is immune - regular cleaning matters more than a 1% efficiency difference on paper.

### Shading
Even partial shade on one panel can cut a whole string's output on a standard string inverter. Efficiency cannot fix shading; layout, microinverters or optimisers do.

### Monsoon and Cloud
Cloudy monsoon days lower generation for everyone; the ~4 units/day/kW figure is an annual average that already accounts for seasonal variation.

## How to Read a Datasheet Before You Buy

Focus on these fields:

- **Rated wattage (Wp)** and **module efficiency (%)** - for space planning.
- **Temperature coefficient of Pmax** - lower magnitude is better for Indian heat.
- **Degradation:** year-1 loss and annual rate (target ~0.5%/year), plus the ~25-year performance warranty end value (~85-88%).
- **ALMM/BIS compliance** - mandatory for subsidy eligibility.
- **Dimensions and weight** - to confirm the panels fit your roof and structure.

## Efficiency Is a Tool, Not a Trophy

Higher efficiency is genuinely useful when your roof is small, when you want to maximise subsidy-eligible capacity, or when heat performance matters. But it is not worth overpaying for on a spacious roof, and it does not change the fundamental generation-per-kW math. Size your system to your bill and roof, pick an ALMM-listed mono PERC panel with a sensible temperature coefficient and warranty, and you will land in the typical residential payback window of 3-5 years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is a good solar panel efficiency for Indian homes?**
Mono PERC panels at ~19-22% efficiency are the standard and more than adequate for Indian rooftops. Higher efficiency mainly helps when roof space is limited; it does not change units generated per kW installed.

**Q: Does higher wattage mean more electricity?**
Not per kW. Generation depends on installed capacity (about 4 units/day/kW), not on whether you use fewer high-wattage or more lower-wattage panels. Higher wattage helps you fit more kW in tight spaces.

**Q: How does heat affect my solar panels in India?**
Output falls as panels heat up, per the temperature coefficient. In Indian summers this can trim generation noticeably, so a panel with a lower (better) temperature coefficient holds up better - a key reason mono PERC is preferred here.

**Q: How much will dust reduce my output?**
Soiling commonly costs 5-15% of output if panels are not cleaned. This affects every panel regardless of rated efficiency, so regular cleaning often matters more than a small efficiency edge on the spec sheet.

**Q: Should I pay extra for the most efficient panels?**
Only if roof space is limited or you want to maximise subsidy-eligible capacity. On a large, unshaded roof, optimise on price per watt instead - overpaying for efficiency can lengthen your payback.

**Q: How many units will my system generate?**
Plan for about 4 units per day per kW - roughly 120 units/month for 1kW, 330-420 for 3kW, and 550-700 for 5kW - averaged across seasons, before soiling and shading losses.
