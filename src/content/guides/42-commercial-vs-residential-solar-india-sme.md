# Commercial vs Residential Solar in India: Key Differences for SMEs

**Meta Description:** Commercial vs residential solar in India: compare cost per watt, subsidy vs tax benefits, net metering, and payback so SMEs choose the right setup in 2026.
**Target Keyword:** commercial vs residential solar India
**Secondary Keywords:** SME rooftop solar, commercial solar cost India, accelerated depreciation solar, PM Surya Ghar residential, solar net metering commercial
**Search Intent:** Comparison

If you own both a home and a small business, or you run a shop with living quarters above it, you have probably wondered whether "solar is solar" or whether the commercial and residential versions really differ. They differ in meaningful ways: cost per watt, the incentives you can claim, net-metering treatment, and how you should size the system. This guide lays out the key differences so SMEs can choose the right setup.

## The big picture: same technology, different economics

The panels, inverters, and mounting are essentially the same across both. Mono PERC modules, ALMM-listed and BIS-compliant, with a performance warranty of about 25 years and roughly 0.5% annual degradation, are the standard in either case. What changes is the financial framework around them: who subsidises what, how you are taxed, and what your DISCOM pays for surplus power.

## Cost per watt

Larger systems cost less per watt because fixed engineering, travel, and paperwork are spread over more capacity.

- **Residential turnkey cost (2026):** about Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW (Rs 40-55 per Wp).
- **Commercial turnkey cost (2026):** about Rs 38-50 per Wp.

Indicative pre-subsidy price points: a 3kW home system runs about Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh, a 5kW about Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh, and a 10kW commercial system about Rs 4.5-6.5 lakh. So per unit of capacity, commercial installs are generally cheaper.

## Incentives: subsidy vs tax benefit

This is the single biggest difference.

- **Residential:** eligible for the **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** central subsidy - Rs 30,000 for 1kW, Rs 60,000 for 2kW, and Rs 78,000 for 3kW and above (capped). The slab is Rs 30,000/kW for the first 2kW, Rs 18,000/kW for the 2-3kW band, and nil above 3kW. Eligible homes can also get up to 300 free units a month, with the subsidy paid by DBT roughly 30-45 days after commissioning. Some states add a top-up.
- **Commercial:** generally **not eligible** for that residential central subsidy. Instead, businesses claim **accelerated depreciation of about 40%** on solar assets (plus normal depreciation), reducing taxable profit and effectively shortening payback. Some states run their own commercial incentives.

So a homeowner leans on upfront subsidy; a business leans on tax savings.

## Taxes and GST

- **Residential:** you pay 12% GST on the system as an end consumer; there is no input tax credit to reclaim.
- **Commercial:** solar modules and systems also attract 12% GST, but a GST-registered business may be able to use input tax credit depending on its circumstances, and it layers the depreciation benefit on top. Always confirm specifics with your chartered accountant.

## Net metering and connection

- **Connection type:** homes are often single-phase; commercial premises are frequently three-phase, so net-metering fees are higher, up to about Rs 15,000 versus Rs 3,000-8,000 for residential.
- **Export rates:** commercial net-metering credit for surplus is often below the retail tariff, which makes self-consumption even more important for businesses. Homes, which consume more in evenings, may export a larger share.
- **Approvals:** commercial applications and DISCOM processes can take longer and involve more scrutiny than residential.

## Consumption patterns change the math

- **Residential load** peaks in mornings and evenings, with lower midday use unless someone is home running ACs or pumps. This creates more daytime surplus for export.
- **Commercial load** (shops, clinics, offices, workshops) peaks during daylight, so a much higher share of generation is self-consumed at the valuable top tariff, which improves the effective return.

Because commercial tariffs are also higher per unit, businesses often see strong payback even without a central subsidy.

## Worked comparison

**Home, 3kW:** cost about Rs 1.9 lakh; PM Surya Ghar subsidy Rs 78,000; net cost roughly Rs 1.1 lakh; generation about 360 units/month; savings around Rs 4,300/month; payback about 3-4 years.

**Shop, 10kW:** cost about Rs 5 lakh; no central subsidy but roughly 40% accelerated depreciation cuts the effective cost through tax saved; generation about 1,200 units/month; savings around Rs 10,800/month at Rs 9/unit commercial tariff; payback about 3-4 years on savings and faster after the depreciation benefit.

Both are excellent investments; they simply get there by different routes.

## Which should an SME choose?

- If the premises is a **home used partly for work**, and the meter is residential, you can access the PM Surya Ghar subsidy - size to your household plus daytime work load.
- If the premises is on a **commercial connection**, plan around accelerated depreciation and higher self-consumption, and consider capex, a loan, or a RESCO model.
- If you have **both**, treat them as separate projects with separate economics rather than one combined system.

Whatever the setup, insist on ALMM-listed modules, an MNRE-empanelled installer, a clear quotation, and an early net-metering application with your DISCOM.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is commercial solar cheaper than residential in India?**
Per watt, yes. Commercial turnkey costs run about Rs 38-50/Wp versus Rs 40-55/Wp residential, mainly because larger systems spread fixed costs over more capacity.

**Can my business claim the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?**
Generally no. That central subsidy (Rs 30,000-78,000) is a residential scheme. Businesses instead rely on accelerated depreciation of about 40% and any state-level commercial incentives.

**Which pays back faster, home or business solar?**
Both typically pay back in a similar 3-5 year range. Businesses often reach the faster end because of higher commercial tariffs, higher daytime self-consumption, and the tax benefit, even without a central subsidy.

**Does net metering work differently for commercial systems?**
Commercial premises are often three-phase, so net-metering fees are higher (up to about Rs 15,000), and surplus export is frequently credited below the retail tariff, making self-consumption more valuable.

**Is the equipment different for commercial and residential?**
The core technology is the same: mono PERC, ALMM-listed, BIS-compliant panels with about 25-year performance warranties. Commercial systems are simply larger and more often three-phase.
