There are dozens of solar panel brands sold in India, and every installer will tell you theirs is the best. Rather than memorise a brand ranking that changes every year, learn how to judge quality and warranty yourself. A panel is a 25-year purchase - the brand's ability to honour a warranty two decades from now matters as much as the spec sheet today.

This guide gives you a practical framework to compare brands and warranties, tuned to Indian conditions and 2026 pricing.

## Why Brand Choice Matters for a 25-Year Asset

Panels rarely fail dramatically; they underperform slowly. A weaker panel might degrade faster than the promised ~0.5% per year, develop micro-cracks in Indian heat, or come from a company that no longer exists when you file a claim. Since modules carry a **~25-year performance warranty**, you are effectively betting on the manufacturer's longevity and service network - not just the glass and silicon.

The good news: the mainstream market has converged on **mono PERC** technology and ALMM compliance, so quality differences between reputable brands are narrower than marketing suggests. Your job is to filter out the weak options and confirm the warranty is real.

## Non-Negotiable: ALMM and BIS Compliance

Before comparing anything else, insist on:

- **ALMM listing:** The panel model must be on MNRE's Approved List of Models and Manufacturers. This is mandatory for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy - a non-ALMM panel disqualifies your entire system from the central subsidy (Rs 30,000/kW up to 2kW, then Rs 18,000/kW to 3kW, capped at Rs 78,000).
- **BIS certification:** Indian standards compliance for safety and quality.

Ask for the exact model number and check it against the current ALMM list. "ALMM brand" is not enough - the specific model must be listed.

## The Two Warranties You Must Separate

Every panel has two distinct warranties. Confuse them and you will misjudge the offer.

### 1. Product (Material) Warranty
Covers manufacturing defects - delamination, faulty cells, frame issues.
- **Typical:** 10-12 years; premium brands offer 15-25 years.
- **What to check:** longer is better; it signals the maker's confidence in build quality.

### 2. Performance (Power Output) Warranty
Guarantees the panel still produces a minimum percentage of rated output over time.
- **Typical:** ~25 years, ending around 85-88% of original output.
- **What to check:** the year-1 output guarantee, the annual degradation rate (target ~0.5%/year), and the end-of-warranty percentage.

**Example:** Brand A offers 12-year product + 25-year performance at 0.5%/year degradation. Brand B offers 15-year product + 25-year performance at 0.45%/year. Brand B is modestly better on paper - but only if both are ALMM-listed and Brand B's service presence in India is solid. A slightly better degradation figure is worthless if you cannot get a claim honoured.

## A Practical Brand Comparison Checklist

Score any quoted brand against these:

- **ALMM + BIS:** is the exact model listed and certified?
- **Manufacturer track record:** years in business, financial stability, India presence.
- **Warranty terms:** product years, performance years, degradation rate, end-of-life output.
- **Service network:** who honours the warranty in India, and how fast?
- **Datasheet specifics:** wattage, efficiency (~19-22% for mono PERC), temperature coefficient (better for Indian heat).
- **Real installations:** ask your installer for local reference sites using the same panel.
- **Documentation:** you receive stamped warranty certificates with serial numbers.

A brand that scores well across these is a safe pick, whether or not it tops a "best brands" list.

## Domestic vs Imported Brands

- **Indian manufacturers:** benefit from ALMM (which favours domestic content), easier local warranty service, and government-scheme alignment. Strong default for subsidised residential systems.
- **Imported brands:** may offer high efficiency, but confirm the model is ALMM-listed (many imports are not) and that Indian warranty support exists.

For a subsidised home system, an ALMM-listed panel with dependable Indian service usually beats a marginally higher-spec import you cannot easily claim against.

## Don't Let Brand Overshadow Installation

Even the best panel underperforms if it is badly installed - poor tilt, shading, undersized cabling, or a mismatched inverter. Quality panels are necessary but not sufficient. Pair a reputable ALMM brand with a competent MNRE-empanelled installer, and remember that a good panel at a fair price (within the typical **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW** turnkey range) will comfortably deliver the roughly 4 units/day/kW and 3-5 year payback that make rooftop solar worthwhile.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Which is the best solar panel brand in India?**
There is no single winner - several reputable ALMM-listed mono PERC brands offer similar quality. Instead of chasing a ranking, choose a brand with strong warranty terms, ALMM/BIS compliance, and reliable Indian service support.

**Q: What warranty should a good solar panel have?**
Look for a product (defect) warranty of at least 10-12 years and a performance warranty of ~25 years ending around 85-88% output, with degradation near 0.5% per year. Longer product warranties signal higher build confidence.

**Q: Does the panel brand affect my subsidy?**
Only through ALMM: the exact panel model must be ALMM-listed (and BIS-certified) for your system to qualify for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy. The brand name itself does not change the subsidy amount, which is based on capacity.

**Q: Are Indian solar brands as good as imported ones?**
For subsidised residential systems, ALMM-listed Indian brands are often the better practical choice - comparable quality, easier local warranty service, and scheme alignment. Confirm any imported model is ALMM-listed before considering it.

**Q: How do I verify a brand's quality claims?**
Check the exact model on the ALMM list, read the datasheet (efficiency, temperature coefficient, degradation), confirm stamped warranty certificates with serial numbers, and ask your installer for local reference installations using the same panel.

**Q: Is a more expensive brand always better?**
Not necessarily. Beyond ALMM compliance and solid warranty terms, quality differences narrow. Overpaying for marginal efficiency gains can hurt your payback; a fairly priced, well-supported ALMM panel is usually the smart buy.
