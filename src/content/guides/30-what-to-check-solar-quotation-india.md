# What to Check in a Solar Quotation Before You Sign in India

A solar quotation is more than a price. Two quotes that both say "3 kW system" can differ by tens of thousands of rupees and by years of performance, depending on the components, warranties, and fine print. Since a rooftop system is a 25-year decision, the quote is where you protect yourself.

This checklist walks through exactly what a good Indian solar quotation should contain, and the red flags that should make you pause before you sign.

## 1. System Size and Generation Estimate

The quote should clearly state the system capacity in kW (DC) and a realistic monthly generation estimate. In India, a well-installed system generates roughly **4 units per day per kW**, so a 3 kW system should be quoted at around 330-420 units per month.

Be skeptical of inflated generation claims. If a vendor promises 500+ units a month from 3 kW, they are overselling.

## 2. Panel Make, Model, and ALMM Listing

The quotation must name the exact panel brand and model, not just "high-efficiency panels." Confirm:

- The module is **mono PERC** (the 2026 residential standard)
- It is **ALMM-listed** and BIS-compliant, which is mandatory to claim the PM Surya Ghar subsidy
- The wattage per panel and total number of panels

A quote that hides the brand is a red flag. You cannot verify warranty or quality without the model number.

## 3. Inverter Brand and Type

The inverter is the heart of the system and typically the first component to need replacement. The quote should specify:

- Brand and model
- Type (string, micro, or hybrid)
- Capacity and whether it is sized correctly for your panels
- Warranty period (usually 5-10 years)

Under-sizing the inverter to cut the price will clip your generation on sunny days.

## 4. Mounting Structure and Materials

Cheap structures rust and void your roof warranty. Look for:

- Galvanised iron (GI) or aluminium structure with a stated warranty
- Cyclone/wind rating appropriate for your region
- Details of waterproofing at roof penetration points

## 5. Warranties in Writing

There are usually three separate warranties. Make sure all appear on the quotation:

- **Panel performance warranty:** around 25 years (typically 80-90% output retained), with degradation of roughly 0.5% per year
- **Panel product warranty:** 10-12 years against defects
- **Inverter warranty:** 5-10 years
- **Workmanship/installation warranty:** ideally 5 years

## 6. Total Price and Per-kW Rate

Calculate the per-kW rate and compare it to the market. In 2026, turnkey residential systems run about **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW**. So a fair 3 kW quote is roughly Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh before subsidy.

- Suspiciously cheap (well below Rs 50,000/kW) usually means low-tier components or missing items.
- Very expensive (above Rs 75,000/kW) needs justification, such as premium panels or a difficult roof.

## 7. What the Subsidy Actually Deducts

Confirm whether the quoted price is before or after subsidy. Under PM Surya Ghar in 2026:

- Rs 30,000 for 1 kW
- Rs 60,000 for 2 kW
- Rs 78,000 for 3 kW and above (capped)

A common tactic is to advertise the "post-subsidy price" while the subsidy is actually credited to your bank later by DBT, 30-45 days after commissioning. You still pay the full amount upfront, then receive the subsidy separately. The quote should make this clear.

## 8. Net Metering and DISCOM Charges

Ask whether net-metering and DISCOM liaison charges are included. These typically run **Rs 3,000-8,000**, and up to about **Rs 15,000 for three-phase connections**. If the quote is silent on this, assume it is extra.

## 9. Scope of Work: What Is and Isn't Included

A complete quote lists everything. Watch for items quietly left out:

- DC and AC cabling, conduits, and earthing
- Lightning arrester and surge protection
- Meter box modifications
- Civil work (if the roof needs a raised structure)
- Transport and installation labour

## 10. Payment Schedule

A reasonable schedule is a booking advance, a payment before/at material delivery, and a balance on commissioning. Avoid vendors demanding 100% upfront. Retain a portion (10-20%) until the system is commissioned and generating.

## 11. Timeline and Commissioning

The quote or accompanying document should give an expected timeline. Realistically, four to eight weeks from order to switch-on in India, given DISCOM feasibility and net-meter waits. See our [Solar Installation Timeline in India](./29-solar-installation-timeline-india) for the full breakdown.

## 12. Vendor Credentials and After-Sales

Confirm the vendor is **MNRE-empanelled** (required for the subsidy), has a GST number, a physical office, and a clear service commitment for maintenance and warranty claims.

## A Worked Comparison

Two quotes for a 3 kW home system:

- **Quote A:** Rs 1.55 lakh. Unnamed panels, no ALMM confirmation, 3-year inverter warranty, net metering "extra." The low price hides low-tier parts.
- **Quote B:** Rs 1.95 lakh. Named ALMM mono PERC panels, 25-year performance warranty, 8-year inverter warranty, net metering and DISCOM charges included, 5-year workmanship warranty.

Quote B costs Rs 40,000 more but is the safer 25-year investment. Both get the same Rs 78,000 subsidy, so the real difference in your pocket is manageable, and Quote B avoids costly failures.

## Red Flags to Walk Away From

- No panel or inverter brand named
- No written warranties
- Pressure to sign "today only" for a discount
- 100% payment demanded upfront
- Generation promises far above 4 units/day per kW
- Not MNRE-empanelled

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a fair price per kW for rooftop solar in India in 2026?**
About Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW for a turnkey residential system. A 3 kW system therefore costs roughly Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh before the subsidy.

**Should I always pick the cheapest quote?**
No. The cheapest quote often uses non-ALMM panels, weak inverters, or excludes net metering and cabling. Compare components and warranties, not just the headline number.

**Is the subsidy deducted from the quote?**
Usually not upfront. You typically pay the full amount, and the central subsidy (up to Rs 78,000 for 3 kW+) is credited to your bank account by DBT 30-45 days after commissioning.

**Why must the panels be ALMM-listed?**
ALMM listing (plus BIS compliance and an MNRE-empanelled vendor) is mandatory to claim the PM Surya Ghar subsidy. Non-listed panels disqualify you.

**Are net-metering charges included in most quotes?**
Not always. Confirm explicitly. They usually cost Rs 3,000-8,000, and up to about Rs 15,000 for three-phase connections.

**How much should I hold back until commissioning?**
Retain roughly 10-20% of the total until the system is inspected, commissioned, and generating, so the vendor has an incentive to finish properly.
