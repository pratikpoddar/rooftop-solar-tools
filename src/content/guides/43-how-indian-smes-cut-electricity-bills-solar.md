For most Indian small and medium enterprises, electricity is one of the largest fixed costs after rent and salaries. Commercial and industrial consumers typically pay Rs 8-12 per unit - far more than the subsidised residential slabs - and tariffs keep rising every year. Rooftop solar is the most reliable way to convert that unpredictable operating expense into a fixed, one-time asset that pays for itself in a few years and then delivers near-free power for two decades.

This guide explains exactly how an SME cuts its bill with solar, what it costs in 2026, and how to think about the numbers.

## Why Solar Works So Well For Businesses

Two factors make the SME case stronger than the residential one:

- **Higher tariffs.** Because commercial buildings pay Rs 8-12/unit, every solar unit displaces expensive grid power, so payback is often faster than for a home.
- **Daytime load.** Shops, clinics, offices and workshops consume most of their electricity between 9 am and 6 pm - exactly when solar generates. This means self-consumption is high and you rely less on net metering to make the economics work.

A rooftop system generates roughly **4 units per day per kW** installed. A 25 kW system therefore produces about 100 units a day, or roughly 3,000 units a month.

## The Math: What You Actually Save

Let's work a concrete example for a mid-size showroom or office.

- **System size:** 25 kW
- **Turnkey commercial cost (2026):** ~Rs 38-50/Wp, so roughly **Rs 10-14 lakh** pre-tax-benefit
- **Generation:** ~100 units/day x 30 = 3,000 units/month
- **Tariff displaced:** Rs 10/unit (typical commercial)
- **Monthly saving:** 3,000 x Rs 10 = **Rs 30,000**
- **Annual saving:** ~Rs 3.6 lakh

At that rate, the system pays back its capital in roughly **3-4 years** even before tax benefits. Add accelerated depreciation (up to ~40% per annum for businesses) and the effective payback can shrink further. For most SMEs the payback lands in the typical **3-5 year** band, after which power is essentially free for the remaining 20+ years of panel life.

## Important: Which Subsidy Applies

A common misconception is that businesses get the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy. They do not. **PM Surya Ghar central subsidy is for residential homes only** (Rs 30,000/kW for the first 2 kW, Rs 18,000/kW for the 2-3 kW band, capped at Rs 78,000 for 3 kW and above).

SMEs instead rely on a different set of financial levers:

- **Accelerated depreciation** on the solar asset (~40% p.a., plus additional depreciation where applicable), which reduces taxable profit substantially in the first year.
- **12% GST** on solar modules and systems, most of which is available as input tax credit for GST-registered businesses.
- **Faster payback** from higher commercial tariffs.

## Net Metering For Commercial Consumers

Net metering lets you export surplus daytime generation to the grid and receive credits against future consumption. Rules vary by DISCOM and by connection type (single vs three-phase), and commercial net-metering caps and settlement terms differ from residential ones.

Key points for SMEs:

- Most SMEs size systems to **maximise self-consumption** rather than heavy export, because that displaces the most expensive units.
- Net-metering and DISCOM application fees are modest - typically **Rs 3,000-8,000**, and up to ~**Rs 15,000** for three-phase connections.
- Confirm your DISCOM's commercial net-metering policy and sanctioned load before finalising system size.

## How To Size And Buy Right

To avoid overspending or undersizing:

1. **Pull 12 months of bills.** Note your average monthly units and your per-unit commercial tariff.
2. **Match daytime load.** Size the system to cover the portion of consumption that happens during sunlight hours.
3. **Check roof space.** You need roughly 80-100 sq ft of shadow-free roof per kW.
4. **Insist on ALMM-listed modules and BIS-compliant equipment.** Mono PERC panels are the current standard, with ~25-year performance warranties and ~0.5%/year degradation.
5. **Get 3 written quotations** and compare on price per Wp, brand, warranty and net-metering support - not just headline price.

### Example: A Small Clinic

A 5 kW system for a clinic costs roughly Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh, generates ~20 units/day (~600/month), and at Rs 10/unit saves about Rs 6,000/month or Rs 72,000/year - a payback of around 4 years, plus depreciation benefits.

## Financing The Purchase

You don't need to pay upfront. Options include:

- **CAPEX with a solar/business loan or EMI**, where you own the asset and claim depreciation.
- **OPEX / RESCO models**, where a developer installs and owns the system and you simply buy the power at an agreed per-unit rate, usually below your grid tariff, with zero upfront cost.

The right choice depends on your tax profile, balance-sheet appetite and how long you plan to occupy the building.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much can an SME save with rooftop solar?**
A well-sized system typically cuts 40-80% of your electricity bill, depending on roof space and daytime load. A 25 kW system can save roughly Rs 30,000/month at a Rs 10/unit commercial tariff.

**Do businesses get the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?**
No. The PM Surya Ghar central subsidy is for residential homes only. Businesses instead benefit from accelerated depreciation, GST input credit and faster payback from higher tariffs.

**What is the payback period for commercial solar?**
Usually 3-5 years, and often at the faster end for SMEs because commercial tariffs (Rs 8-12/unit) are higher than residential ones.

**Will solar cover my entire bill?**
Rarely 100%, because you also draw power at night. Most SMEs offset the daytime portion, which is the bulk of their load, and use net metering for surplus.

**How much roof space do I need?**
Roughly 80-100 sq ft of shadow-free roof per kW, so about 2,000-2,500 sq ft for a 25 kW system.

**How long do the panels last?**
Around 25 years of useful life, with mono PERC modules degrading about 0.5% per year and carrying ~25-year performance warranties.
