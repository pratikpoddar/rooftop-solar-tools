# Step-by-Step Rooftop Solar Installation Process in India

Installing rooftop solar in India is more than bolting panels to a roof. It is a sequence of technical, financial, and regulatory steps involving your installer, your DISCOM, and the PM Surya Ghar portal. Knowing the full process helps you avoid delays, spot a disorganised vendor, and receive your subsidy on time. Here is exactly what happens, step by step.

## Overview: What the Journey Looks Like

From first enquiry to a live, subsidy-credited system usually takes **4 to 8 weeks**, depending on DISCOM speed and documentation. The core phases are:

1. Registration and vendor selection
2. Site survey and system design
3. Application and DISCOM feasibility approval
4. Procurement and physical installation
5. Net-metering application and meter installation
6. DISCOM inspection and commissioning
7. Subsidy disbursal via DBT

## Step 1: Register on the PM Surya Ghar Portal

For residential systems seeking the central subsidy, the journey starts at **pmsuryaghar.gov.in**. You:

- Register with your state, DISCOM, and consumer number.
- Log in and submit an application for rooftop solar.
- Choose from **MNRE-empanelled vendors** listed for your DISCOM.

Using an empanelled vendor and **ALMM-listed modules** is mandatory to qualify for the subsidy, so do not skip this even if a cheaper non-listed installer approaches you.

## Step 2: Site Survey and System Design

Your chosen vendor visits to assess:

- Usable, shadow-free roof area (about 80-100 sq ft per kW).
- Roof structure and load-bearing capacity.
- Your sanctioned load and consumption pattern.
- Optimal orientation (ideally south-facing) and tilt.

They then propose a system size, equipment list, layout, and a detailed quotation. This is where you confirm the kW, panel brand, inverter type, and total cost. For a typical home this lands on a **3 kW system at Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh pre-subsidy**.

## Step 3: DISCOM Feasibility Approval

Your application goes to the DISCOM for a **technical feasibility check**. They verify:

- Your connection details and sanctioned load.
- That the proposed capacity is acceptable on the local network.
- Whether a load enhancement is needed for larger systems.

Once approved, you receive a feasibility clearance to proceed. This step can take a few days to a couple of weeks depending on your DISCOM.

## Step 4: Procurement and Installation

With approval in hand, the vendor procures equipment and schedules installation. Physical work is typically completed in **1 to 3 days** for a residential system and includes:

- Mounting the galvanised structure on the roof.
- Fixing and wiring the solar panels.
- Installing the inverter (string or hybrid) and safety gear (isolators, earthing, surge protection).
- Connecting AC/DC cabling to your distribution board.

A quality installer uses proper earthing, lightning protection, and weatherproof cable routing. Ask for a layout drawing and a list of exact serial numbers of installed ALMM panels.

## Step 5: Net-Metering Application and Meter Installation

To export surplus power and get credited, you need **net metering**. The vendor typically helps you:

- Apply to the DISCOM for a net-metering connection.
- Pay net-metering / processing fees, usually **Rs 3,000-8,000**, and up to around **Rs 15,000 for three-phase** connections.
- Get a **bi-directional meter** installed by the DISCOM, replacing your old meter.

This meter records both import and export, which is the basis for your future bills.

## Step 6: DISCOM Inspection and Commissioning

Before the system goes live officially, the DISCOM conducts an **inspection** to verify:

- The installation matches the approved design and safety norms.
- Correct meter installation and wiring.
- ALMM compliance of the modules.

On passing, the DISCOM issues a **commissioning certificate** and your system is officially connected to the grid. Your panels may already be generating during testing, but this certificate is the milestone that unlocks the subsidy.

## Step 7: Subsidy Disbursal via DBT

After successful inspection and commissioning, the central subsidy is paid via **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)** to your registered bank account, typically within **30 to 45 days**. The central subsidy amounts are:

- **Rs 30,000** for 1 kW
- **Rs 60,000** for 2 kW
- **Rs 78,000** for 3 kW and above (capped)

Some **states add their own top-up subsidy**, credited separately per state rules. Keep your bank details on the portal accurate to avoid DBT delays.

## A Realistic Timeline Example

For a Bengaluru homeowner installing a 3 kW system:

- Week 1: Portal registration, vendor selection, site survey.
- Week 2-3: Feasibility approval and equipment procurement.
- Week 3-4: Installation over 1-2 days.
- Week 4-6: Net-metering application, bi-directional meter, DISCOM inspection.
- Week 6-8: Commissioning certificate, then subsidy DBT within 30-45 days.

Net outlay after the Rs 78,000 subsidy is roughly Rs 87,000-1.32 lakh, with a payback of about 3-5 years.

## Documents You Will Need

- Latest electricity bill and consumer number.
- Identity and address proof.
- Bank account details for DBT.
- Roof ownership proof or society NOC (for shared roofs).
- Passport-size photos as required by the portal.

## Tips to Avoid Delays

- Pick an MNRE-empanelled vendor with ALMM modules from the start.
- Keep the portal bank details exactly matching your account.
- Respond quickly to DISCOM queries.
- Insist on a written project timeline and warranty documents.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does rooftop solar installation take in India?
End to end, usually 4-8 weeks. Physical installation is 1-3 days; the rest is approvals, net metering, inspection, and commissioning.

### Do I have to use the PM Surya Ghar portal?
For the central residential subsidy, yes. You must register at pmsuryaghar.gov.in and use an MNRE-empanelled vendor with ALMM-listed modules.

### When do I receive my subsidy?
Via DBT to your bank account, typically 30-45 days after successful DISCOM inspection and commissioning.

### What is a bi-directional meter?
A net meter that records both the power you import from the grid and the surplus you export, so you are billed on the net units.

### Can I use any installer I like?
For subsidy eligibility you must use an MNRE-empanelled vendor. Non-empanelled installers may be cheaper but disqualify you from the central subsidy.
