Choosing the right system size is the single most important decision in going solar. Too small, and you keep paying big grid bills; too large, and you overspend on capacity you cannot use or export profitably. The good news: you can size a rooftop system yourself in a few minutes using your electricity bill, your roof, and a couple of Indian rules of thumb.

This guide walks through the calculation step by step, with 2026 costs and the subsidy limits that shape the sweet spot for most homes.

## The Simple Rule: Match Generation to Consumption

The goal for a grid-tied home on net metering is to generate roughly what you consume over a year. Two Indian planning figures make this easy:

- **Generation:** about **4 units per day per kW** (so 1kW makes ~120 units/month; 3kW makes ~330-420 units/month).
- **Roof space:** roughly 80-100 sq ft of shadow-free area per kW.

Size the system so its annual generation is close to your annual consumption - not much more, because export credit under net metering is usually less valuable than self-use, and subsidy caps out at 3kW.

## Step 1: Find Your Monthly Consumption

Look at your electricity bill for "units consumed" (kWh). Average a few months to smooth out seasonal swings (fans and ACs push summer usage up).

- Note your **monthly units** (e.g. 400 units/month).
- Note your **connection type** (single-phase or three-phase) and **sanctioned load** - your system should generally stay within it.

## Step 2: Convert Units to System Size

Divide your monthly consumption by the ~120 units/month each kW produces:

**System size (kW) = Monthly units / 120**

Examples:
- 250 units/month -> ~2kW
- 360 units/month -> ~3kW
- 600 units/month -> ~5kW

**Worked example:** A family using 400 units/month divides 400 by 120 = ~3.3kW. A 3kW system generating ~330-420 units/month covers most of their usage, sits right at the subsidy cap, and keeps cost efficient. They might round to 3kW rather than 3.5kW to maximise subsidy value per rupee.

## Step 3: Check Your Roof Space

Multiply your target kW by ~80-100 sq ft:

- **2kW** -> ~160-200 sq ft
- **3kW** -> ~240-300 sq ft
- **5kW** -> ~400-500 sq ft

Only count **shadow-free** area (avoid water tanks, parapets, and tree shade). If your roof cannot fit your consumption-based size, use higher-efficiency mono PERC panels to squeeze in more kW, or size to what the roof allows.

## Step 4: Factor In the Subsidy Sweet Spot

The PM Surya Ghar subsidy strongly rewards systems up to 3kW:

- **1kW:** Rs 30,000
- **2kW:** Rs 60,000
- **3kW and above:** Rs 78,000 (capped)

The slab logic is Rs 30,000/kW for the first 2kW, Rs 18,000/kW for the 2-3kW band, and **nil above 3kW**. So a 3kW system captures the maximum Rs 78,000, but a 5kW system gets the *same* Rs 78,000 - the extra 2kW earns no central subsidy.

This makes **3kW the value sweet spot** for many Indian homes: it covers typical consumption, fits common roofs, and maxes out the subsidy. Go larger only if your consumption genuinely warrants it and you value the additional self-generation despite no extra subsidy.

## Step 5: Estimate the Cost

At 2026 turnkey pricing of **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW**:

| Size | Pre-subsidy cost | Central subsidy | Approx net |
|------|------------------|-----------------|-----------|
| 1kW | Rs 60,000-80,000 | Rs 30,000 | ~Rs 30,000-50,000 |
| 2kW | Rs 1.1-1.5 lakh | Rs 60,000 | ~Rs 50,000-90,000 |
| 3kW | Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh | Rs 78,000 | ~Rs 87,000-1.32 lakh |
| 5kW | Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh | Rs 78,000 | ~Rs 1.72-2.72 lakh |
| 10kW | Rs 4.5-6.5 lakh | Rs 78,000 | ~Rs 3.72-5.72 lakh |

Add one-time net-metering/DISCOM charges of roughly Rs 3,000-8,000 (up to ~Rs 15,000 for three-phase). Some states add their own top-up subsidy, improving the net cost further. Across sizes, residential payback typically lands in the **3-5 year** range.

## Common Sizing Mistakes to Avoid

- **Oversizing beyond consumption:** you pay for capacity that only earns lower export credit and no extra subsidy above 3kW.
- **Undersizing to save money:** you keep a large grid bill and lengthen your effective payback on the money you did spend.
- **Ignoring roof shade:** a shadow-free layout matters more than raw roof area.
- **Exceeding sanctioned load** without a load-enhancement request to your DISCOM.
- **Sizing on one summer bill:** always average across seasons.

## Quick Sizing Cheat Sheet

- Monthly units / 120 = target kW.
- Confirm roof fits (~80-100 sq ft per kW, shade-free).
- Stay at or below 3kW to maximise subsidy value unless consumption clearly demands more.
- Keep within sanctioned load (or request enhancement).
- Budget Rs 55,000-70,000/kW plus small DISCOM fees.

Get these five right and your system will match your bill, capture the full subsidy you are entitled to, and pay for itself within a few years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do I calculate what size solar system I need?**
Divide your average monthly consumption (units from your bill) by about 120, since each kW generates roughly 120 units/month in India. For example, 360 units/month suggests a 3kW system. Then confirm your roof has enough shade-free space.

**Q: What is the most common home solar size in India?**
3kW is a very common choice for Indian homes - it covers typical consumption of around 300-400 units/month, fits most roofs (~240-300 sq ft), and captures the maximum Rs 78,000 central subsidy under PM Surya Ghar.

**Q: Is it worth installing more than 3kW?**
Only if your consumption clearly warrants it. The central subsidy caps at Rs 78,000 (3kW and above), so extra capacity earns no additional subsidy and any surplus is exported at lower credit. Size to your usage rather than overbuilding.

**Q: How much roof space do I need?**
Roughly 80-100 sq ft of shadow-free area per kW - about 240-300 sq ft for a 3kW system. Only count space free of shade from tanks, walls and trees.

**Q: What if my roof is too small for my consumption?**
Use higher-efficiency mono PERC panels to fit more kW in the same area, or install the largest system your roof allows and offset the rest from the grid. High efficiency matters most on tight roofs.

**Q: Will a bigger system shorten my payback?**
Not necessarily. Payback is typically 3-5 years across sizes, but oversizing beyond your consumption (and above the 3kW subsidy cap) can lengthen it, because the extra generation earns only export credit and no extra subsidy.
