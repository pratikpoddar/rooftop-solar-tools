For an Indian business, the sticker price of a rooftop solar system is not the real cost. Between accelerated depreciation and GST input credit, a large chunk of the investment comes back through tax savings - often making solar one of the most tax-efficient capital purchases a company can make. This guide explains how those benefits work in 2026 and how to estimate them for your own system.

Note upfront: these benefits apply to businesses. The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy is a separate scheme for residential homes only and is not available to commercial installations.

## What Is Accelerated Depreciation?

Depreciation lets a business write off the cost of an asset against its taxable profit over the asset's life. **Accelerated depreciation** allows solar assets to be written off much faster than normal plant and machinery.

For solar power generating equipment, businesses can claim depreciation at roughly **40% per annum** (plus additional depreciation where applicable in the year of installation). Because you deduct a large share of the asset's value in the first year or two, your taxable profit falls sharply, and so does your tax outgo.

In simple terms: a rupee of depreciation at a 25-30% effective tax rate puts roughly 25-30 paise back in your pocket as tax saved.

## Worked Example: A 25 kW System

Assume a mid-size SME installs a 25 kW rooftop system.

- **System cost (2026 commercial):** ~Rs 12 lakh (within the Rs 10-14 lakh band for 25 kW)
- **Depreciation rate:** ~40% per annum
- **Assumed effective tax rate:** ~30%

First-year depreciation alone would be around **Rs 4.8 lakh** (40% of Rs 12 lakh), which at a 30% tax rate saves roughly **Rs 1.44 lakh in tax in year one**. Over the first couple of years, cumulative depreciation writes off the bulk of the asset value, translating into significant tax savings that shorten the effective payback well beyond the energy savings alone.

This is why many profitable SMEs treat solar not just as an energy decision but as a deliberate tax-planning move before financial year end.

## GST Input Tax Credit

GST on solar modules and systems is currently **12%**. For a GST-registered business, most of this GST paid on the purchase is available as **input tax credit (ITC)**, which can be set off against your output GST liability.

Practical points:

- Ensure your installer issues a proper **GST invoice** with your GSTIN so you can claim ITC.
- ITC eligibility depends on the nature of your business and how the asset is used; confirm with your accountant.
- The net effect is that the "real" cost of the system, after ITC, is lower than the headline price for most eligible businesses.

## Stacking The Benefits

The financial advantages compound for a business:

- **Accelerated depreciation** reduces taxable profit and tax outgo in the early years.
- **GST input credit** reduces the effective purchase price.
- **Higher commercial tariffs (Rs 8-12/unit)** mean each solar unit saves more than it would for a home, so energy payback is fast.
- **Net metering** credits any surplus you export.

Together, these often bring the effective payback into the typical **3-5 year** range - sometimes faster for high-tariff, high-daytime-load businesses - after which the system delivers near-free power for the balance of its ~25-year life.

## Who Can Claim These Benefits

Accelerated depreciation and GST input credit are available to businesses that own the asset (the CAPEX model). If you instead use an OPEX or RESCO arrangement - where a developer owns the system and sells you power - the developer claims the depreciation, not you, and you simply enjoy a lower per-unit tariff with no upfront cost.

So the choice of ownership model directly determines who captures the tax benefit. Businesses with taxable profits and appetite to own usually prefer CAPEX to capture depreciation; those wanting zero capex and no asset on the books prefer OPEX/RESCO.

## Documentation And Compliance

To claim benefits smoothly, keep:

- The **GST tax invoice** with your GSTIN.
- Proof of **commissioning and capitalisation date** (this determines the depreciation year).
- Records showing the asset is used for business.
- **ALMM-listed module** and BIS-compliance documentation, which also protects warranty claims.

Always run the exact depreciation percentages, additional depreciation eligibility and tax rate through your Chartered Accountant, since these depend on your entity type, turnover and the applicable finance-act provisions for the year.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the depreciation rate on solar for businesses?**
Solar power equipment qualifies for accelerated depreciation of roughly 40% per annum, plus additional depreciation where applicable in the year of installation.

**How much tax can I actually save?**
On a Rs 12 lakh, 25 kW system, first-year depreciation of ~Rs 4.8 lakh can save around Rs 1.44 lakh in tax at a 30% rate, with more in the following year.

**Can I claim GST input credit on solar?**
Yes. GST on solar systems is 12% and most GST-registered businesses can claim it as input tax credit, provided the installer issues a proper GST invoice with your GSTIN.

**Do homeowners get accelerated depreciation?**
No. Depreciation is a business benefit. Homeowners instead use the PM Surya Ghar residential subsidy, which does not apply to businesses.

**Does accelerated depreciation apply under OPEX/RESCO?**
No. Under OPEX/RESCO the developer owns the asset and claims depreciation; you benefit through a lower power tariff instead.

**Should I install before year end?**
Many businesses do, to capture first-year depreciation in the current financial year - but always confirm timing with your CA.
