# Solar Panel Maintenance in India: A Complete Homeowner's Checklist

The good news about rooftop solar is that it has no moving parts and asks very little of you. The systems are built to run for 25 years with only light, regular care. The bad news is that "very little" is not "nothing", and a neglected system quietly loses output year after year until you wonder why your bills crept back up.

This checklist covers everything an Indian homeowner needs to keep a rooftop system generating at its best, how often each task is needed, and what it should cost.

## Why Maintenance Matters

A solar panel degrades naturally by only about **0.5% per year**, so after 25 years it should still produce around 85-88% of its original output. That slow, predictable decline is normal and covered by the performance warranty.

What is not normal is losing 15-25% of output in a single year to dust, shading, loose connections, or an inverter fault. Almost all of that loss is preventable with routine maintenance. On a 3 kW system generating 330-420 units a month, a 20% dust loss can cost you 70-80 units every month, which adds up fast against rising tariffs.

## The Maintenance Calendar at a Glance

- **Weekly to fortnightly:** Check the inverter display and app for normal generation
- **Monthly (dry season) / after dust storms:** Clean the panels
- **Quarterly:** Visual inspection of panels, cables, and mounting
- **Before and after monsoon:** Deep check of waterproofing, earthing, and drainage
- **Annually:** Professional service / AMC visit

## 1. Monitor Generation Regularly

Your inverter or its app shows daily and monthly generation. Get familiar with what a good day looks like for your system, then watch for drops.

- A clear day in your city should produce close to 4 units per day per kW.
- A sudden fall of 20% or more (not explained by weather) signals a problem.
- Many inverters flag faults with error codes; note them and share with your installer.

Regular monitoring is the single most valuable maintenance habit because it catches issues early.

## 2. Keep the Panels Clean

Dust, bird droppings, pollen, and pollution form a film that blocks sunlight. In dry, dusty regions and cities with heavy air pollution, this is the biggest cause of lost output.

- **How often:** Monthly in the dry season; more often after dust storms or in high-dust areas. The monsoon rinses panels naturally, reducing the need.
- **How:** Early morning or evening, with plain water and a soft brush or microfibre cloth. Avoid hard water spots by wiping dry where possible.
- **Safety first:** Never clean panels on a wet or sloped roof without secure footing.

For a full method, see [How to Clean Solar Panels Safely (and How Often) in India](./32-how-to-clean-solar-panels-india).

## 3. Inspect for Shading

Shade that did not exist at installation can appear as trees grow or neighbours build. Even partial shade on one panel can drag down a whole string.

- Trim overhanging branches
- Watch for new water tanks, dish antennas, or construction casting shadows
- Note the times of day when shadows fall and discuss re-positioning with your installer if severe

## 4. Check Cabling, Connectors, and Earthing

Quarterly, do a visual check (from a safe position) of:

- DC and AC cables for wear, rodent damage, or exposure to sun
- Connectors and junction boxes for looseness or discolouration (a sign of heat)
- The earthing and lightning arrester connections

Loose or corroded connections reduce output and are a fire risk. Anything suspicious should be left to a technician, not handled yourself.

## 5. Look After the Inverter

The inverter is the component most likely to need attention over the system's life.

- Keep it in a shaded, ventilated spot, free of dust build-up on vents
- Ensure nothing blocks airflow around it
- Note any error codes or unusual noise
- Expect it to be replaced once during the system's 25-year life (warranty typically 5-10 years)

## 6. Mounting Structure and Waterproofing

The structure and roof penetrations take a beating from sun, wind, and rain.

- Check bolts and clamps are tight after strong winds
- Look for rust on the GI/aluminium structure
- Before the monsoon, confirm waterproofing around roof mounts is intact to prevent leaks
- Ensure roof drainage near the array is clear

## 7. Seasonal Care in India

Indian conditions demand a bit of seasonal attention:

- **Summer:** Peak dust and heat; clean more often and ensure inverter ventilation.
- **Monsoon:** Rain cleans panels but tests waterproofing and earthing; check for leaks and standing water. See [Monsoon and Solar: How Rooftop Systems Perform in India's Rainy Season](./34-monsoon-solar-performance-india).
- **Post-monsoon:** Inspect for any water ingress, rust, or loosened fixings.

## 8. Annual Professional Service (AMC)

Once a year, have a qualified technician do a full check: electrical testing, connector torque, inverter diagnostics, and structural inspection. Many homeowners buy an **Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC)**.

- **Typical AMC cost:** roughly Rs 1,500-5,000 per year for a residential system, depending on size and inclusions
- **What it covers:** scheduled cleaning visits, inspections, and priority fault attendance

For a 3 kW system, an AMC is a small price against the value of the electricity you would lose from an undetected fault.

## A Simple Homeowner Routine

- **Every week:** Glance at the inverter app; is generation normal?
- **Every month (dry season):** Clean the panels or have them cleaned
- **Every quarter:** Visual inspection of panels, cables, structure
- **Twice a year:** Pre- and post-monsoon deep check
- **Once a year:** Professional service / AMC visit

Follow this and your system should comfortably deliver its expected output across its full 25-year performance warranty.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much maintenance does a rooftop solar system need in India?**
Very little. Mainly regular cleaning, occasional visual inspections, and one annual professional service. There are no moving parts, so upkeep is minimal but not zero.

**How often should I clean my solar panels?**
Monthly during the dry season, and more often in dusty areas or after dust storms. The monsoon rinses panels naturally, so cleaning frequency drops during the rains.

**What does a solar AMC cost in India?**
An Annual Maintenance Contract for a residential system typically costs around Rs 1,500-5,000 per year, covering scheduled cleaning, inspections, and fault support.

**Will I ever need to replace the panels or inverter?**
Panels are built for about 25 years and rarely need replacement. The inverter is more likely to be replaced once during the system's life, usually after its 5-10 year warranty.

**How do I know if my system needs attention?**
Watch the generation figures. A drop of 20% or more that weather does not explain, or an inverter error code, means it is time to inspect or call your installer.

**Does dust really reduce output that much?**
Yes. Accumulated dust and grime can cut output by 15-25% in dusty or polluted areas, which is why regular cleaning is the highest-value maintenance task.
