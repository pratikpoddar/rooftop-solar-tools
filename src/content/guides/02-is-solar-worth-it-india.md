"Is solar worth it?" is the question almost every Indian homeowner asks before spending on a rooftop system. The honest answer: for most homes with a decent roof and a monthly bill above Rs 2,500, yes, it usually is. But the details matter. This guide walks through the real numbers in 2026 so you can decide with confidence.

## The short answer

Rooftop solar in India is worth it when three things line up:

- You own a roof with reasonable, unshaded south-facing space.
- Your monthly electricity bill is meaningfully high (Rs 2,500 or more).
- You plan to stay in the home long enough to cross the payback point (typically 3-5 years).

If all three are true, solar usually delivers over a decade of near-free power after payback, plus a hedge against rising tariffs.

## What it costs upfront in 2026

Residential rooftop solar in 2026 costs roughly **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW** on a turnkey basis (about Rs 40-55 per watt). Indicative pre-subsidy prices:

- **1kW:** Rs 60,000-80,000
- **2kW:** Rs 1.1-1.5 lakh
- **3kW:** Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh
- **5kW:** Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh

On top of the hardware, budget for net-metering and DISCOM charges of roughly Rs 3,000-8,000 (up to about Rs 15,000 for a three-phase connection).

## How much the subsidy cuts the price

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, launched in February 2024, offers a central subsidy for residential systems:

- **1kW:** Rs 30,000
- **2kW:** Rs 60,000
- **3kW and above:** Rs 78,000 (capped)

The slab works out to Rs 30,000 per kW for the first 2kW, Rs 18,000 per kW for the 2-3kW band, and nothing above 3kW. Some states add their own top-up on top of this. The subsidy reaches your bank account by Direct Benefit Transfer, usually 30-45 days after your DISCOM commissions the system.

## The savings side: what you get back

The value of solar comes from the electricity you no longer buy. Each 1kW generates about 4 units per day, so:

- A **3kW** system produces roughly 330-420 units a month.
- At an effective tariff of Rs 8 per unit, that is about Rs 2,600-3,400 in monthly savings.
- Over a year, that is roughly Rs 32,000-40,000 back in your pocket.

Eligible households can also receive up to 300 free units per month under the scheme, and rising tariffs mean your savings grow every year electricity gets more expensive.

## A worked example: a 3kW home in Bengaluru

Take a family with a Rs 4,500 monthly bill and a good roof:

- **Pre-subsidy cost:** ~Rs 1.9 lakh
- **PM Surya Ghar subsidy:** Rs 78,000
- **Net cost:** ~Rs 1.12 lakh (plus ~Rs 5,000 net-metering charges)
- **Annual savings:** ~Rs 38,000
- **Payback:** roughly 3-4 years

After that, the system keeps generating for 20-plus more years, with panels degrading only about 0.5% per year and carrying a ~25-year performance warranty. Compared with a fixed deposit, the effective return is far higher and, unlike interest, the savings are effectively tax-free.

## When solar may NOT be worth it (yet)

Be realistic. Solar makes less sense if:

- Your roof is heavily shaded, north-facing, or has very little usable space.
- Your monthly bill is very low (below ~Rs 1,500), stretching the payback well beyond 5 years.
- You rent, or plan to sell/move within a year or two, though solar can raise property value.
- Your building is a shared apartment where roof rights are unclear, unless the society goes solar collectively.

## Beyond the money: other reasons it pays off

- **Tariff hedge:** Grid tariffs keep climbing; solar locks in a large chunk of your usage at today's cost.
- **Property value:** A commissioned rooftop system can make a home more attractive to buyers.
- **Reliability of savings:** Unlike market investments, sunshine and your own consumption are predictable.
- **Low maintenance:** Occasional cleaning and an annual check are usually all that is needed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the payback period for home solar in India?**
For a well-sized residential system with subsidy, payback is typically 3-5 years, after which the electricity is effectively free for the rest of the panels' 25-year life.

**How much can a 3kW system save per month?**
Around 330-420 units of generation, worth roughly Rs 2,600-3,400 a month at an Rs 8/unit tariff, depending on your city and consumption pattern.

**Is the subsidy worth applying for?**
Yes. At Rs 78,000 for a 3kW system, the PM Surya Ghar subsidy can cut your net cost by a third or more. You must use an MNRE-empanelled vendor and ALMM-listed panels to qualify.

**Is solar a better return than a fixed deposit?**
Generally yes. The savings-based return on a subsidised system typically beats FD interest, and unlike FD interest, the benefit is not taxed.

**Do I need a big bill for solar to make sense?**
A monthly bill of Rs 2,500 or more usually makes solar clearly worthwhile. Below that, the payback stretches out and the case weakens.

**Will I still get a bill after going solar?**
Often a small one, covering fixed charges and any net units you draw beyond what your system exports. Many homes see their energy charges fall close to zero.
