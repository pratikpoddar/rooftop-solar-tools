# Documents Required for Rooftop Solar Subsidy in India

**Search Intent:** Transactional

Getting the central subsidy under the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana is mostly a paperwork exercise. If your documents are ready and correct, the application on pmsuryaghar.gov.in moves quickly; if a name or number does not match, the subsidy can stall for weeks. This checklist tells you exactly what to keep ready before you apply, why each document matters, and the small mismatches that trip most homeowners up.

Under the scheme (launched February 2024, targeting 1 crore homes), the central subsidy is Rs 30,000 for 1kW, Rs 60,000 for 2kW, and Rs 78,000 for 3kW and above (capped). The money is paid by Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) to your bank account roughly 30-45 days after your DISCOM inspects and commissions the system - which is why your bank and identity documents must be flawless.

## The Core Checklist at a Glance

Keep clear scans or photos (usually under 5MB, JPG/PDF) of the following:

- **Identity proof:** Aadhaar card (primary), plus PAN card
- **Latest electricity bill:** for the connection where the plant will be installed
- **Proof of ownership / roof rights:** property tax receipt, sale deed, or allotment letter
- **Bank account proof:** cancelled cheque or passbook front page for DBT
- **Passport-size photograph:** of the applicant
- **Consumer number / connection details:** printed on your electricity bill
- **Mobile number linked to Aadhaar:** for OTP verification

That is the backbone. Below, each item is explained so you know what "correct" looks like.

## Identity and KYC Documents

**Aadhaar card** is the anchor of your application. The name on Aadhaar should match the name on the electricity bill and bank account. Your mobile number must be linked to Aadhaar because the portal sends an OTP to verify you.

**PAN card** is used for KYC and for the DBT bank verification. Make sure the name spelling is consistent with Aadhaar - even "Kumar" vs "K." can cause a rejection at the bank-verification step.

If the electricity connection is in a family member's name (a common situation), the subsidy is paid to that consumer's linked account. Decide upfront whose name the application will run under, and use that person's Aadhaar, PAN and bank details throughout.

## Electricity Connection Documents

**Latest electricity bill** proves you are a genuine DISCOM consumer and confirms your sanctioned load and consumer category (residential). From the bill the portal reads your:

- Consumer number / account ID
- DISCOM name and division
- Sanctioned load (in kW)
- Connection type (single-phase or three-phase)

Your rooftop system size should be in line with your sanctioned load. If you want a system larger than your sanctioned load, you may need a load-enhancement request with the DISCOM first - a step your MNRE-empanelled vendor usually handles.

**Example:** A Pune homeowner applying for a 3kW system needs a bill showing a residential connection of at least 3kW sanctioned load. With the 3kW plant costing roughly Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh pre-subsidy, the Rs 78,000 central subsidy lands in the same bank account whose passbook they upload - so that passbook must match their Aadhaar name.

## Property and Roof Ownership Proof

You must show you have the right to install on the roof. Acceptable documents usually include:

- Latest property tax receipt in the applicant's name
- Registered sale deed or allotment letter
- For apartments/societies: a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the housing society or RWA

Tenants generally cannot claim the subsidy on their own; the owner (or a formally authorised owner) must apply. For shared or society rooftops, the NOC and a resolution allowing the installation are often required before the DISCOM approves net metering.

## Bank Details for the Subsidy (DBT)

The subsidy is credited by DBT, so the bank document is non-negotiable:

- **Cancelled cheque** clearly showing account number, IFSC and account holder name, or
- **Bank passbook front page** with the same details

The account should be active, in the applicant's name, and ideally the same account seeded for Aadhaar DBT. A frozen, dormant, or minor's account can delay the transfer even after commissioning.

## Documents Your Vendor Handles (Technical Stage)

Once you register and choose an MNRE-empanelled vendor, a second set of documents is generated during installation and inspection. You do not create these, but you should collect copies:

- **Net metering application** to the DISCOM
- **Installation report / plant details:** panel make and model (must be ALMM-listed), inverter details, capacity
- **Warranty certificates:** ~25-year performance warranty on modules, inverter warranty
- **Commissioning certificate** signed off after DISCOM inspection
- **Net meter installation confirmation**

These matter later: for warranty claims, for property records, and if you ever sell the house. Insist your installer hands over a full document folder.

## Common Mistakes That Delay the Subsidy

- **Name mismatch** across Aadhaar, bill, PAN and bank account - the single biggest cause of stalled DBT.
- **Aadhaar not linked to mobile,** so OTP verification fails at registration.
- **Bill in a relative's name** but application filed under a different person.
- **Choosing a non-empanelled vendor** or **non-ALMM panels,** which makes the plant ineligible for subsidy.
- **Blurry or oversized uploads** that get rejected at document verification.
- **Wrong DISCOM division** selected during registration.

Fixing these before you apply saves the most time. The scheme is generous - up to 300 free units a month for eligible households and a subsidy that pushes residential payback to a typical 3-5 years - but only if the paperwork clears cleanly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is the single most important document for the solar subsidy?**
Your Aadhaar, because it drives identity verification and the DBT that pays the subsidy. Ensure the name and linked mobile number are correct and consistent with your electricity bill and bank account.

**Q: Can I apply if the electricity bill is in my father's or spouse's name?**
Yes, but the application, Aadhaar, PAN and bank account should all belong to that same consumer, since the subsidy is credited to the connection holder's account. Alternatively, transfer the connection first.

**Q: Do I need a property document if I own a flat in an apartment?**
Usually yes - property tax proof plus a No Objection Certificate from your society or RWA, since the roof is shared. Requirements vary slightly by DISCOM.

**Q: Are the panel and inverter documents my responsibility?**
No. Your MNRE-empanelled vendor generates the technical documents (ALMM panel details, net metering, commissioning). But always collect copies for warranty and resale.

**Q: How soon after submitting documents do I get the subsidy?**
The DBT is typically credited about 30-45 days after the DISCOM completes inspection and commissioning, provided your bank details match your KYC.

**Q: What if my documents have a name spelling mismatch?**
Correct it before applying - update either the bill, bank record, or PAN so all names align with Aadhaar. A mismatch is the most common reason a subsidy transfer fails.
