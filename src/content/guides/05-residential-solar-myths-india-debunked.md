# 10 Myths About Residential Solar in India - Debunked

Misinformation stops many Indian homeowners from going solar. "It is too expensive." "It does not work in the rains." "The panels need constant cleaning." Most of these beliefs are outdated or simply wrong. Here are 10 common myths about residential solar in India, debunked with 2026 facts.

## Myth 1: Solar is too expensive for a normal home

**Reality:** Prices have fallen sharply. In 2026, residential rooftop solar costs about Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW turnkey. A 3kW system runs Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh before subsidy, and the PM Surya Ghar subsidy of Rs 78,000 brings the net cost down to roughly Rs 90,000-1.3 lakh. With payback of 3-5 years, it is an affordable, income-generating asset, not a luxury.

## Myth 2: The subsidy is a gimmick that never actually pays out

**Reality:** The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, launched in February 2024, credits the subsidy directly to your bank account by Direct Benefit Transfer, typically 30-45 days after your DISCOM commissions the system. As long as you use an MNRE-empanelled vendor with ALMM-listed panels and complete the process on pmsuryaghar.gov.in, the money comes through.

## Myth 3: Solar does not work during the monsoon or on cloudy days

**Reality:** Panels still generate on cloudy days, just less. Systems are sized across the whole year, so strong generation in sunny months offsets weaker monsoon output. With net metering, surplus units you export in good months carry forward as credits to cover cloudy stretches. Expect about 4 units per day per kW as a yearly average.

## Myth 4: You need a huge roof

**Reality:** You need roughly 100 square feet of shade-free space per kW. A 3kW system fits in about 300 square feet, well within most independent homes and many terraces. Higher-efficiency monocrystalline panels pack more power into less space.

## Myth 5: Solar panels need constant, expensive maintenance

**Reality:** Rooftop solar is low-maintenance. Occasional cleaning to remove dust and an annual inspection are usually enough. There are no moving parts in the panels. Rain often does much of the cleaning for you. Maintenance costs are minor compared with the savings.

## Myth 6: Panels will not last long

**Reality:** Quality panels carry a performance warranty of around 25 years and degrade only about 0.5% per year. That means after two decades they still produce roughly 90% of their original output. Solar is a long-term asset, not a short-lived gadget.

## Myth 7: Solar will completely disconnect me from the grid

**Reality:** Most home systems are on-grid and stay connected. Solar powers your home by day and exports surplus; you draw from the grid at night. You are not cut off, you simply buy far fewer units. Only off-grid systems are fully independent, and they are used mainly where the grid is absent.

## Myth 8: Net metering is complicated and not worth it

**Reality:** Net metering is straightforward. A bi-directional meter records the units you import and export, and your DISCOM bills you only for the net. Export more than you import in a month and the credit carries forward. It is the core mechanism that makes your bill shrink.

## Myth 9: Solar does not add value to my property

**Reality:** A commissioned rooftop solar system can make a home more attractive to buyers, since it comes with built-in savings and a hedge against rising tariffs. It is increasingly seen as a desirable, future-ready feature.

## Myth 10: A power cut means my solar keeps my lights on

**Reality:** This one is the opposite of what many assume. A standard on-grid system shuts off during a grid outage for safety, so it does not provide backup by itself. If you want power during cuts, you need a hybrid system with a battery. Knowing this upfront prevents disappointment.

## A quick reality check with numbers

Consider a Hyderabad home with a Rs 4,000 monthly bill and a 3kW system:

- **Pre-subsidy cost:** ~Rs 1.9 lakh
- **Subsidy:** Rs 78,000
- **Net cost:** ~Rs 1.12 lakh
- **Generation:** ~330-420 units a month
- **Payback:** ~3-4 years

The numbers, not the myths, show why so many households are switching.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is home solar really affordable in India now?**
Yes. After the Rs 78,000 subsidy on a 3kW system, net cost is around Rs 90,000-1.3 lakh, with a 3-5 year payback. Falling hardware prices have made it mainstream.

**Does solar work in Indian weather conditions?**
Yes. Panels perform across seasons; strong summer output and net-metering credits offset weaker monsoon generation. Plan for about 4 units per day per kW on average.

**Will I get free electricity with solar?**
Eligible households can receive up to 300 free units a month under PM Surya Ghar, and net metering can push energy charges close to zero for well-sized systems.

**Do panels really last 25 years?**
Quality ALMM-listed panels come with around a 25-year performance warranty and lose only ~0.5% output a year, so they keep producing for decades.

**Is maintenance a big burden?**
No. Periodic cleaning and an annual check are typically all that is needed. There are no moving parts to wear out.

**Does solar power my home during outages?**
Only if it is a hybrid system with a battery. A standard on-grid system switches off during a grid failure for safety.
