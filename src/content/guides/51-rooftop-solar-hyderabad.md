Hyderabad's hot, sun-rich climate and steadily rising tariffs make rooftop solar a sensible investment for homeowners and small businesses alike. Independent houses across Jubilee Hills, Kukatpally, Gachibowli and the outer suburbs typically have the flat, open terraces that solar loves. This guide walks through 2026 costs, the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, TGSPDCL net metering and how installation actually happens.

## Why Hyderabad is well suited to solar

- **Strong irradiance.** Hyderabad enjoys long, clear sunny spells for most of the year. Systems generate roughly 4 units per day per kW.
- **Flat, spacious roofs.** Many independent homes have terraces large enough for 3-5 kW without shading trouble.
- **Rising bills.** As slab tariffs climb, self-generated power becomes more valuable each year.
- **Subsidy support.** The central PM Surya Ghar scheme reduces upfront cost significantly for residential users.

A 3 kW system in Hyderabad commonly produces about 330-420 units a month, enough to cover a large part of a typical family's consumption.

## Rooftop solar costs in Hyderabad (2026)

Turnkey residential systems in 2026 cost about Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW (around Rs 40-55 per watt) using ALMM-listed mono PERC panels and a quality inverter. Indicative pre-subsidy figures:

- **1 kW:** Rs 60,000-80,000
- **2 kW:** Rs 1.1-1.5 lakh
- **3 kW:** Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh
- **5 kW:** Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh
- **10 kW:** Rs 4.5-6.5 lakh

SME and commercial systems, usually larger, are closer to Rs 38-50 per watt. Add roughly Rs 3,000-8,000 for net metering and DISCOM processing, or up to about Rs 15,000 for a three-phase connection.

## PM Surya Ghar subsidy in Hyderabad

The central subsidy applies to residential rooftop systems across Telangana:

- **1 kW:** Rs 30,000
- **2 kW:** Rs 60,000
- **3 kW and above:** Rs 78,000 (capped)

That is Rs 30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and Rs 18,000 per kW for the third kW, with nothing beyond 3 kW. Apply on pmsuryaghar.gov.in, choose an MNRE-empanelled vendor using ALMM-listed modules, and the amount is credited to your bank by DBT about 30-45 days after TGSPDCL commissions the system. Eligible households can also receive up to 300 free units a month. Any additional Telangana state incentives, where available, would be over and above this central benefit.

## Net metering with TGSPDCL

Most of Hyderabad is served by TGSPDCL (the Telangana Southern Power Distribution Company). Under net metering:

- Solar power you export by day is credited against the grid units you consume, and you are billed only on the net.
- System capacity is generally linked to your sanctioned load, so size accordingly.
- Three-phase homes and businesses have slightly higher metering and connection charges.
- Apply for net metering alongside your subsidy application; the DISCOM inspects and replaces your meter before commissioning.

Keep your latest bill, sanctioned-load details and identity/ownership documents ready, since these feed both the subsidy and net-metering processes.

## Is your roof ready?

- **Space.** Budget roughly 80-100 sq ft of shadow-free roof per kW. A 3 kW system needs about 250-300 sq ft.
- **Orientation.** South-facing, tilted mounting maximises output; flat terraces use tilted frames.
- **Shading.** Water tanks, parapets and neighbouring buildings can cut generation, so a proper site survey matters.
- **Structure.** Confirm the roof can bear the lightweight mounting and panels; almost all RCC terraces can.

## A worked example: 3 kW home in Kukatpally

Take a family in an independent house in Kukatpally on TGSPDCL supply:

- **Pre-subsidy cost:** about Rs 1.85 lakh
- **PM Surya Ghar subsidy:** Rs 78,000
- **Net outlay:** about Rs 1.07 lakh (plus ~Rs 5,000 net metering)
- **Generation:** roughly 360 units a month
- **Bill offset:** at prevailing residential slab rates, savings of around Rs 2,500-3,200 a month, or Rs 30,000-38,000 a year

That puts payback in the region of 3-4 years. With a 25-year performance warranty and only about 0.5% degradation per year, the system produces cheap power for two decades after break-even.

## The installation process, step by step

1. **Site survey and quotation** from an MNRE-empanelled installer.
2. **Portal registration** on pmsuryaghar.gov.in and DISCOM feasibility approval.
3. **Installation** of panels, inverter and safety equipment (a day or two of work).
4. **Net-meter inspection and commissioning** by TGSPDCL.
5. **Subsidy disbursal** to your bank account by DBT within about 30-45 days of commissioning.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which DISCOM handles rooftop solar in Hyderabad?**
Most of Hyderabad falls under TGSPDCL, which processes your net-metering application and installs the bidirectional meter.

**How much does a 3 kW system cost after subsidy?**
Roughly Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh before subsidy, less Rs 78,000, leaving a net outlay near Rs 1 lakh plus small net-metering charges.

**How many units will I generate?**
About 4 units per day per kW, so a 3 kW system yields roughly 330-420 units a month depending on season and shading.

**Is the PM Surya Ghar subsidy available in Telangana?**
Yes. The central subsidy of up to Rs 78,000 applies to residential systems; apply via pmsuryaghar.gov.in using an empanelled vendor.

**What is the payback period in Hyderabad?**
Typically 3-4 years for residential systems, thanks to strong generation and rising tariffs.

**Do I need a battery?**
For grid-connected homes with net metering, a battery is optional. It adds cost and is mainly worth it if you face frequent outages.
