If you are considering solar panels for your home for the first time, the jargon can feel overwhelming: kW, ALMM, net metering, PERC, DISCOM. This beginner's guide explains everything you need in plain language, with 2026 India figures, so you can move from curious to confident.

## Why Indian homeowners are switching to solar

Three forces are driving the shift:

- **Rising tariffs:** Grid electricity keeps getting costlier every year.
- **Government support:** The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidises residential systems and offers eligible homes up to 300 free units a month.
- **Falling hardware prices:** Panels now cost far less than a decade ago, making payback of 3-5 years realistic.

Put simply, solar turns your roof into a money-saving asset for 25 years.

## Types of solar panels used in Indian homes

You will mainly encounter these:

- **Monocrystalline PERC:** The standard for Indian homes in 2026. Higher efficiency, better in limited roof space, and the default choice most installers quote.
- **Polycrystalline:** Older, slightly cheaper, lower efficiency. Increasingly rare in new residential installs.
- **Bifacial:** Capture light on both sides for a small generation boost; useful on reflective or open rooftops.

Whatever the type, insist that panels are **ALMM-listed and BIS-compliant**. This matters for quality and is required to claim the subsidy. Good panels carry a performance warranty of around 25 years and degrade only about 0.5% per year.

## The parts of a home solar system

A complete home system includes:

- **Panels (modules)** on the roof.
- **Inverter** to convert DC to usable AC power.
- **Mounting structure** to hold panels at the right angle.
- **Net meter** installed by your DISCOM to track import and export.
- **Cabling, safety devices and earthing.**

## What size system does your home need?

Sizing starts with your electricity usage. A practical method:

1. Check your average monthly units from past bills.
2. Remember each 1kW generates about 4 units per day, so around 120 units a month.
3. Divide your monthly units by 120 to estimate the kW you need.

For example, a home using 360 units a month needs roughly a 3kW system. As a rough guide:

- **Small homes / low bills:** 1-2kW
- **Typical urban family:** 3kW
- **Large homes with AC load:** 5kW or more

A 3kW system produces about 330-420 units a month, enough for many urban families.

## How much do home solar panels cost in India?

In 2026, residential rooftop solar costs roughly **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW** turnkey (about Rs 40-55 per watt). Indicative pre-subsidy prices:

- **1kW:** Rs 60,000-80,000
- **2kW:** Rs 1.1-1.5 lakh
- **3kW:** Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh
- **5kW:** Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh

Add Rs 3,000-8,000 for net-metering and DISCOM charges (up to about Rs 15,000 for three-phase connections).

## The subsidy that makes it affordable

The PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana central subsidy for residential systems:

- **1kW:** Rs 30,000
- **2kW:** Rs 60,000
- **3kW and above:** Rs 78,000 (capped)

The subsidy is calculated at Rs 30,000/kW for the first 2kW and Rs 18,000/kW for the third kW, with nothing beyond 3kW. Several states add their own top-up. After your DISCOM commissions the system, the amount is credited to your bank account by Direct Benefit Transfer, usually within 30-45 days.

## A real example: a first-time buyer in Ahmedabad

A family using around 380 units a month with a Rs 4,200 bill:

- **System:** 3kW monocrystalline PERC
- **Pre-subsidy cost:** ~Rs 1.9 lakh
- **Subsidy:** Rs 78,000 (plus any Gujarat state top-up)
- **Net cost:** ~Rs 1.12 lakh
- **Generation:** ~330-420 units a month
- **Payback:** roughly 3-4 years

After payback, they enjoy two more decades of low-cost power.

## How to get started

1. Gather 6-12 months of electricity bills to understand your usage.
2. Register at pmsuryaghar.gov.in and shortlist MNRE-empanelled vendors.
3. Get at least two written quotations specifying panel brand, ALMM listing, inverter and price.
4. Compare warranties and after-sales support, not just price.
5. Approve the design, let the vendor handle DISCOM approvals, and schedule installation.
6. After commissioning and net-meter installation, claim your subsidy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many solar panels does a home need?**
It depends on system size. A 3kW system uses roughly 6-8 modern panels, though the exact count varies with panel wattage and roof layout.

**Which panel type is best for Indian homes?**
Monocrystalline PERC is the standard in 2026 for its efficiency and space savings. Ensure the panels are ALMM-listed and BIS-compliant.

**How much roof space do I need?**
Plan for roughly 100 square feet of shade-free space per kW, so about 300 square feet for a 3kW system.

**What is the total cost after subsidy for a 3kW system?**
Around Rs 90,000-1.3 lakh after the Rs 78,000 central subsidy, before any state top-up, plus small net-metering charges.

**How long do home solar panels last?**
Quality panels last around 25 years with only ~0.5% annual degradation, well beyond the 3-5 year payback period.

**Do I need permission to install solar?**
Yes, your DISCOM must approve the grid connection and install a net meter. Using an MNRE-empanelled vendor keeps the process and subsidy on track.
