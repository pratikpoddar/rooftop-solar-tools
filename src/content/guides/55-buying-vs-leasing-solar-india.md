# Buying vs Leasing Solar in India: Which Model Saves More?

Should you buy a rooftop solar system outright or lease it? For most Indian homeowners the answer is clear, but for many SMEs the choice is genuinely open. This guide compares the two ownership models, their costs, subsidy eligibility and long-term savings, so you can pick the one that saves you more.

## The two models, defined

- **Buying (CAPEX / ownership).** You pay for the system upfront (or via a loan) and own it. You keep all the electricity savings, claim the subsidy and, for businesses, tax benefits. You are responsible for maintenance.
- **Leasing (OPEX / RESCO / PPA).** A third-party developer installs and owns the system on your roof at no upfront cost to you. You either pay a fixed monthly lease or buy the generated power at an agreed per-unit rate under a power purchase agreement (PPA). The developer handles maintenance.

The core trade-off is simple: buying means higher upfront cost but far greater lifetime savings; leasing means no upfront cost but you share the benefits with the developer.

## Upfront cost and who pays

Under the **buying** model in 2026, residential turnkey systems cost about Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW (Rs 40-55 per watt); commercial systems run nearer Rs 38-50 per watt. Indicative pre-subsidy prices:

- **3 kW (home):** Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh
- **5 kW (home/small shop):** Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh
- **10 kW (SME):** Rs 4.5-6.5 lakh

Under **leasing**, your upfront cost is effectively zero. The developer funds the installation and recovers it through your monthly lease or per-unit PPA tariff over the contract, typically 10-25 years.

If cash flow is the only constraint, a solar loan on a bought system (available as EMIs) is often a better middle path than leasing, because you still own the asset and keep the subsidy.

## Subsidy and tax eligibility: the deciding factor

This is where the models diverge sharply.

- **PM Surya Ghar subsidy (residential).** The central subsidy of Rs 30,000 (1 kW), Rs 60,000 (2 kW) and Rs 78,000 (3 kW and above) goes to the **system owner**. When you buy, that is you, credited by DBT about 30-45 days after commissioning. When you lease, the developer owns the system and captures the subsidy. For homeowners, this alone usually makes buying the clear winner.
- **Business tax benefits.** Companies that buy can claim accelerated depreciation on the asset and offset it against taxable profit. Under a lease/RESCO model, the developer claims depreciation instead, though your lease/PPA rate reflects some of that benefit.

For a home, leasing means giving up the subsidy that makes solar so attractive. For an SME, the calculation depends on whether you value the depreciation and full savings (buy) or prefer zero capex and outsourced risk (lease).

## Lifetime savings compared

Because a bought system belongs to you, you keep 100% of the bill savings for the full 25-year panel life, long after payback. A leased system shares those savings with the developer for the duration of the contract.

- **Buying:** Payback is typically 3-5 years for residential systems. After that, electricity is almost free for the remaining ~20 years, with only ~0.5% annual degradation.
- **Leasing:** You save from day one with no capital, but your savings are capped at the discount the PPA/lease offers versus the grid tariff, usually a modest margin, for the whole contract.

Over 25 years, ownership almost always delivers more total rupees saved. Leasing wins only when you cannot or do not want to invest capital, or when you want someone else to carry performance and maintenance risk.

## A worked example: 5 kW system

Compare a 5 kW system for a home or small shop generating about 600 units a month (roughly 4 units/day per kW).

**Buying:**
- Pre-subsidy cost: about Rs 3 lakh
- PM Surya Ghar subsidy: Rs 78,000
- Net outlay: about Rs 2.22 lakh (plus net metering)
- Annual bill savings: roughly Rs 55,000-70,000
- Payback: about 3-4 years; you then keep all savings for ~20 more years

**Leasing (illustrative PPA):**
- Upfront cost: Rs 0
- You buy the same ~600 units/month from the developer at a per-unit rate below your grid tariff
- Annual saving to you: typically a fraction of the full savings above, for the whole contract
- No subsidy or depreciation benefit to you; the developer keeps those

Over the asset's life, the bought system saves far more, even after accounting for its upfront cost.

## When each model makes sense

**Buy (CAPEX) if you:**
- Want the PM Surya Ghar subsidy (essential for homeowners)
- Can pay upfront or take a solar loan/EMI
- Want maximum lifetime savings and to own the asset
- Are a business that can use accelerated depreciation

**Lease (OPEX/RESCO) if you:**
- Want zero upfront investment
- Prefer the developer to own performance and maintenance risk
- Are an SME with large roof space but limited capex appetite
- Value predictable monthly costs over maximum savings

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can homeowners get the subsidy if they lease?**
No. The PM Surya Ghar subsidy goes to the system owner. Under a lease, the developer owns the system and receives the subsidy, so homeowners should generally buy.

**Is leasing ever better for a home?**
Rarely. Because you forfeit the subsidy and share savings, leasing suits homeowners only if they cannot invest any capital and prefer no maintenance responsibility.

**What is a RESCO model?**
Renewable Energy Service Company: a developer installs and owns the system on your roof and sells you the power under a PPA, or leases the system, with no upfront cost to you.

**Which model is better for SMEs?**
It depends. Buying maximises savings and lets you claim accelerated depreciation; leasing preserves capital and outsources risk. Larger businesses with steady cash flow often prefer buying.

**Can I buy with a loan instead of paying cash?**
Yes. Solar loans and EMIs let you own the system (and keep the subsidy) while spreading the cost, often a better deal than leasing.

**What happens at the end of a lease?**
Depending on the contract, you may extend, have the system removed, or buy it at a residual value. Always read the end-of-term clauses before signing.
