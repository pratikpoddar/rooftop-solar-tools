When you spend around Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh on a 3 kW rooftop system, a fair question is: how long will it actually last, and how much will it still produce a decade or two from now? The reassuring answer is that solar panels are one of the longest-lived pieces of equipment you will ever install on your home, and their decline is slow, small, and predictable.

This guide explains realistic lifespan, degradation rates, warranties, and what output to expect over the years in Indian conditions.

## How Long Do Solar Panels Last?

Modern solar panels are built for a working life of around **25 years or more**. That is not a hard cut-off; most panels keep generating well beyond 25 years, just at gradually reduced output. The 25-year figure is the standard **performance warranty** period, not an expiry date.

By comparison, the inverter has a shorter life and will likely be replaced once during the system's lifetime.

## Understanding Degradation

No panel produces exactly the same power at year 20 as on day one. This gradual decline is called **degradation**, and for modern mono PERC panels it runs at roughly **0.5% per year**.

That is a very slow decline. Here is what it means in practice:

- **Year 1:** ~100% output
- **Year 5:** ~97-98%
- **Year 10:** ~95%
- **Year 20:** ~90%
- **Year 25:** ~85-88%

So after a full 25 years, a good panel still delivers around 85-88% of its original power. This is why most manufacturers guarantee 80-90% output at the end of the warranty term.

## The Two Warranties You Get

Panels come with two distinct warranties, and it helps to understand both:

- **Performance warranty (~25 years):** Guarantees the panel will still produce a minimum percentage of rated output (commonly 80-90%) at year 25, allowing for the ~0.5%/year degradation.
- **Product warranty (~10-12 years):** Covers manufacturing defects and physical failure.

Always confirm both are stated in your quotation. See [What to Check in a Solar Quotation Before You Sign in India](./30-what-to-check-solar-quotation-india).

## What Affects Lifespan in Indian Conditions

Indian rooftops face heat, dust, humidity, and monsoon. These influence how well panels age:

- **Heat:** Very high temperatures slightly increase degradation. Good airflow behind the panels helps.
- **Quality of panels:** ALMM-listed, BIS-compliant mono PERC panels from reputable brands age more predictably than cheap, unlisted modules.
- **Installation quality:** Poor mounting, weak structures, and bad cabling shorten real-world life far more than the cells themselves do.
- **Maintenance:** Regular cleaning and inspections keep panels near their rated curve; neglect causes losses that mimic degradation.

The single biggest lever is buying quality panels and having them installed properly. Cutting corners on either is what actually shortens a system's useful life.

## Degradation vs. Dirt: Don't Confuse Them

Homeowners often blame "old panels" for low output when the real cause is dust or shading. Remember:

- True degradation is only about 0.5% per year, so you would barely notice it year to year.
- A sudden 15-25% drop is almost always **dust, shading, or a fault**, not aging.

If output falls noticeably, clean and inspect first. See [Why Is My Solar System Underperforming?](./33-solar-system-underperforming-troubleshooting-india) before assuming your panels have worn out.

## What Happens After 25 Years?

At the end of the warranty period, your panels do not stop working. They continue generating at 85-88% of original output and keep declining slowly. Many owners run them for 30 years or more.

- You may choose to keep them as-is for continued savings.
- You may upgrade to newer, higher-efficiency panels if you want more output from the same roof space.
- End-of-life panels can be recycled; India's e-waste framework increasingly covers solar modules.

## A Worked Example: 3 kW System Over 25 Years

A 3 kW system in a sunny Indian city generating about 12 units/day (330-420 units/month) at the start:

- **Year 1:** ~4,300-5,000 units generated
- **Year 10 (~95%):** still around 4,100-4,750 units
- **Year 25 (~86%):** around 3,700-4,300 units

Even in its final warranty year, the system produces the large majority of its original electricity. Against a typical residential payback of **3-5 years**, that means roughly two decades of near-free power after the system has paid for itself.

## Making Your Panels Last

To get the full lifespan:

- Buy **ALMM-listed, BIS-compliant** panels from established brands with clear warranties.
- Use an **MNRE-empanelled** installer with a solid mounting structure and quality cabling.
- **Clean** regularly and inspect quarterly.
- Do a **pre-monsoon check** each year for waterproofing and earthing.
- Keep the **inverter** cool and ventilated; expect one replacement over the life.
- Keep warranty documents safe so you can claim if performance falls below the guaranteed curve.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long do solar panels last in India?**
Around 25 years or more. The 25-year figure is the performance warranty period, and most panels keep generating beyond it at slightly reduced output.

**What is the degradation rate of solar panels?**
Roughly 0.5% per year for modern mono PERC panels. That means about 90% output at year 20 and 85-88% at year 25.

**Will my panels stop working after 25 years?**
No. They continue generating at around 85-88% of original output and decline slowly from there. Many systems run 30 years or more.

**Does India's heat reduce panel lifespan?**
Very high temperatures slightly increase degradation, but good airflow behind the panels and quality modules keep this minimal. Heat is a modest factor, not a dealbreaker.

**My output dropped a lot. Are my panels worn out?**
Almost certainly not. Degradation is only ~0.5% per year, so a large drop points to dust, shading, or a fault. Clean and inspect before blaming age.

**Do inverters last as long as panels?**
No. Inverters typically carry a 5-10 year warranty and are usually replaced once during the system's 25-year life, unlike the panels themselves.
