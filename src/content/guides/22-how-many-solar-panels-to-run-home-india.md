# How Many Solar Panels to Run a Home in India? (Appliance-by-Appliance)

The most common question Indian homeowners ask before going solar is deceptively simple: "How many panels do I actually need?" The honest answer is that you do not size a system by panel count. You size it by how many units (kWh) of electricity your home consumes, then convert that into kilowatts (kW), and only at the end into a number of panels. This guide walks through the logic appliance by appliance so you can arrive at a realistic number.

## First, Panels Are Just a Way to Reach a kW Target

Modern residential panels in India in 2026 are typically mono PERC modules rated between 540 Wp and 575 Wp. As a working rule:

- A **1 kW** system needs roughly **2 panels** of ~550 Wp.
- A **3 kW** system needs roughly **5-6 panels**.
- A **5 kW** system needs roughly **9-10 panels**.
- A **10 kW** system needs roughly **18-20 panels**.

So the real task is figuring out the kW, not the panel count. Once you know the kW, the installer picks the exact panel count based on the wattage they stock.

## Step 1: Know Your Monthly Consumption

Look at your electricity bill and find your average monthly units (kWh). Most Indian urban homes fall between 200 and 600 units per month. In India, **each 1 kW of rooftop solar generates roughly 4 units per day**, or about **120 units per month** (this varies by city and season). A 3 kW system therefore produces roughly **330-420 units per month**.

To roughly size your system:

- Monthly consumption ÷ 120 = approximate kW needed.
- 300 units/month ÷ 120 ≈ **2.5 kW**.
- 500 units/month ÷ 120 ≈ **4-4.5 kW**.

## Step 2: The Appliance-by-Appliance View

If you do not trust your bill, build consumption from appliances. Here are typical daily energy figures for an Indian household:

- **Ceiling fans (3-4):** 300-450 Wh/day
- **LED lights (8-10):** 300-500 Wh/day
- **Refrigerator (double door):** 1,000-1,500 Wh/day
- **1.5-ton split AC (5-6 hrs):** 4,000-6,000 Wh/day
- **Television + set-top box:** 300-500 Wh/day
- **Washing machine (alternate days):** 400-600 Wh/day
- **Water pump (motor):** 500-800 Wh/day
- **Laptop, phone charging, router:** 300-500 Wh/day
- **Geyser (winter, 30-45 min):** 1,500-2,000 Wh/day

### Example: A Typical 3-BHK Family

- Fans, lights, fridge, TV, router, chargers: ~3.5 units/day
- One 1.5-ton AC for 5 hours: ~5 units/day
- Water pump + washing machine: ~1 unit/day
- **Total: roughly 9.5 units/day, or ~285 units/month.**

At ~120 units per kW per month, this home needs about **2.5 kW**, so a **3 kW system (5-6 panels)** is the practical fit. Rounding up to 3 kW is smart because it hits the maximum PM Surya Ghar central subsidy slab.

## Step 3: Match to Standard System Sizes and Costs

Here is a realistic 2026 mapping for Indian homes. Turnkey residential prices are roughly **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW** before subsidy.

| Home type | Monthly units | System size | Panels (~550 Wp) | Indicative pre-subsidy cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small flat, no AC | 100-150 | 1 kW | 2 | Rs 60,000-80,000 |
| Compact home, 1 AC occasional | 200-300 | 2 kW | 4 | Rs 1.1-1.5 lakh |
| 3-BHK, 1-2 ACs | 300-420 | 3 kW | 5-6 | Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh |
| Large home, multiple ACs | 500-700 | 5 kW | 9-10 | Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh |
| Villa / heavy load | 900+ | 10 kW | 18-20 | Rs 4.5-6.5 lakh |

### The Subsidy Sweet Spot

Under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, the central subsidy is **Rs 30,000 for 1 kW, Rs 60,000 for 2 kW, and Rs 78,000 for 3 kW and above (capped)**. Because the subsidy is capped at 3 kW, most homeowners who consume 250-450 units land on a 3 kW system to maximise the benefit. Above 3 kW there is no additional central subsidy, so you pay full turnkey price for the extra capacity.

## Step 4: Do You Need Panels for the AC?

Air conditioners drive most of the confusion. A single 1.5-ton AC running 5-6 hours a day can consume 5-6 units, which alone justifies roughly 1.5 kW of extra solar. If you plan to add ACs or run them more in summer, size up front rather than retrofitting later. On an on-grid net-metered system, you do not need panels to "cover" the AC minute-by-minute; the grid balances your usage and net metering credits your daytime surplus against evening consumption.

## Step 5: Leave Room for the Future

Two practical adjustments most installers recommend:

- **Add 10-15% headroom** if you expect an EV, a new AC, or a growing family.
- **Check your sanctioned load** with your DISCOM. Your solar system's capacity is often capped at or near your sanctioned load, so you may need to apply for a load enhancement for larger systems.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many panels do I need to run a 1.5-ton AC?
The AC itself adds roughly 5-6 units/day, needing about 1.5 kW extra, or about 3 additional panels. But on a net-metered on-grid system you size the whole home together, not per appliance.

### Can 3 kW (5-6 panels) run a full home?
Yes, for most 3-BHK homes consuming 300-420 units/month with one or two ACs used moderately. Heavy AC use or multiple ACs pushes you toward 5 kW.

### How many units will a 3 kW system generate?
Roughly 330-420 units per month, based on about 4 units per day per kW, varying by city, season, and shading.

### Will more panels lower my bill to zero?
With net metering and correct sizing, a well-matched system can offset most or all of your consumption. Oversizing beyond your usage gives diminishing returns because export tariffs are usually lower than retail tariffs.

### Do panel counts change with brand?
Yes. Higher-wattage panels mean fewer panels for the same kW. A 3 kW system may use 5 panels of 575 Wp or 6 of 500 Wp; the kW and generation stay the same.
