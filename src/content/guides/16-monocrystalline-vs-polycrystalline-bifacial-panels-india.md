# Monocrystalline vs Polycrystalline vs Bifacial Solar Panels: Which to Choose in India

If you are buying rooftop solar in India, the panel-type decision is simpler than most quotations make it sound. The market has largely settled on one answer for homes - but the trade-offs are worth understanding so you can read a quote, judge value, and avoid paying for the wrong technology on the wrong roof.

This guide compares the three panel types you will actually be offered - monocrystalline (mono PERC), polycrystalline, and bifacial - on efficiency, cost, space, and real Indian rooftop conditions.

## The Three Panel Types in Plain Terms

- **Monocrystalline (mono PERC):** Made from a single silicon crystal, uniform black cells. Highest efficiency in the mainstream, best in limited space and heat. This is the default for Indian homes in 2026.
- **Polycrystalline:** Made from melted silicon fragments, bluish speckled cells. Cheaper historically but lower efficiency; now largely phased out of new residential offerings.
- **Bifacial:** Usually mono cells with a glass or transparent back that also captures light reflected off the surface below. Can add extra generation - but only when the mounting and surface suit it.

All three, to qualify for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, must be **ALMM-listed** and BIS-compliant. Non-ALMM panels are cheaper but make your system ineligible for the central subsidy, which is a false economy.

## Efficiency and Wattage

Efficiency decides how many panels fit your roof and how much you generate per square metre.

- **Mono PERC:** ~19-22% cell efficiency; typical modules 540-590Wp and rising. Standard choice.
- **Polycrystalline:** ~15-17% efficiency; you need more panels and more roof for the same kW.
- **Bifacial:** Front side similar to mono; rear side can add 5-20% extra output in ideal conditions (reflective, elevated mounting).

In India's generation math, roughly **4 units per day per kW** is a reasonable planning figure regardless of panel type - panel choice mainly affects how much roof that kW consumes, not the kW-to-units ratio. A 3kW system generates about 330-420 units a month whether the cells are mono or bifacial; bifacial can nudge the upper end when mounted well.

## Cost per Watt in 2026

Residential turnkey systems in 2026 cost roughly **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW** (about Rs 40-55 per Wp) installed, including panels, inverter, structure, wiring and labour.

- **Polycrystalline** shaves a little off the panel line item but is rarely offered new, and the roof-space penalty often cancels the saving.
- **Mono PERC** sits in the middle and is the value standard.
- **Bifacial** typically adds a premium and needs elevated mounting to earn it back - which most flat residential roofs do not provide.

**Example:** A 3kW mono PERC system priced at ~Rs 1.8 lakh pre-subsidy, minus the Rs 78,000 central subsidy, comes to about Rs 1.02 lakh net. Swapping to bifacial might push the gross to ~Rs 2.0 lakh; unless your roof lets the rear side work, you are paying more for generation you will not fully capture.

## How Each Type Handles Indian Conditions

Indian rooftops mean heat, dust, monsoon and often tight space. That shapes the choice.

### Heat
High cell temperatures reduce output. Mono PERC panels generally have better temperature coefficients than older poly panels, so they hold up better through Indian summers.

### Limited roof space
Most urban homes are space-constrained. Higher-efficiency mono panels give you more kW on the same roof - decisive in cities like Mumbai, Bengaluru or Chennai where roof area, not budget, is the binding constraint.

### Dust and monsoon
All panels lose output when soiled; none of the three is immune. Bifacial's rear gain also drops if the reflecting surface is dirty or shaded. Regular cleaning matters more than panel type here.

### Bifacial's sweet spot
Bifacial shines on **elevated ground mounts, carports, or reflective white/light rooftops with an air gap** beneath. On a typical flush-mounted residential rooftop pressed close to a dark surface, the rear-side bonus is small - so the premium rarely pays for a standard home.

## Which Should You Choose?

- **Most Indian homes:** Mono PERC. Best balance of efficiency, price, heat tolerance and space use. It is the safe, subsidy-eligible default.
- **Tight roofs / high bills:** Mono PERC, and prioritise the highest-wattage ALMM modules you can fit.
- **Ground mount, carport, or reflective terrace with proper elevation:** Bifacial can be worth the premium.
- **Polycrystalline:** Only if offered at a genuinely large discount and you have plenty of roof space - increasingly hard to justify and rarely available new.

Whatever the cell type, quality of the specific panel brand, its warranty, and correct installation matter more than the label. Look for a ~25-year performance warranty and expect about 0.5% output degradation per year.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is monocrystalline always better than polycrystalline?**
For nearly all Indian homes in 2026, yes - mono PERC offers higher efficiency, better heat performance and better use of limited roof space, at a price that has converged with poly. Poly only makes sense with a large discount and abundant space.

**Q: Are bifacial panels worth it for a rooftop home?**
Usually not on a standard flush-mounted roof. Bifacial needs elevated, reflective, unshaded mounting to capture rear-side light. On ground mounts and carports it can add meaningful output; on typical terraces the premium rarely pays back.

**Q: Do all three types qualify for the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?**
Only if the modules are ALMM-listed and BIS-compliant, regardless of cell type. Always confirm the exact panel model is on the ALMM list before ordering.

**Q: Will a higher-efficiency panel generate more units per kW?**
Not materially - about 4 units per day per kW is the planning norm across types. Higher efficiency means more kW fits your roof, so you install a bigger system in the same space.

**Q: How long do these panels last?**
All three types typically carry a ~25-year performance warranty with roughly 0.5% degradation per year, so they still produce around 85-88% of rated output after 25 years if maintained.

**Q: Does panel type change my payback period?**
Only slightly. Residential payback is typically 3-5 years driven by system size, tariff and subsidy. Paying a bifacial premium without the right mounting can lengthen payback rather than shorten it.
