# CAPEX vs OPEX/RESCO Solar Models for Indian SMEs Explained

When an Indian SME decides to go solar, the biggest financial fork in the road is not which panel to buy - it is how to pay for the system. The two dominant models are **CAPEX** (you buy and own the plant) and **OPEX / RESCO** (a developer owns the plant and sells you the power). Each changes who pays upfront, who claims tax benefits, and who carries the risk. This guide compares them clearly so you can pick the right one.

## CAPEX: You Own The System

Under the CAPEX (capital expenditure) model, your business buys the rooftop solar system outright - with cash, a business loan, or EMI - and owns it for its full ~25-year life.

**How it works:**
- You pay the full turnkey cost (commercial systems run ~Rs 38-50/Wp in 2026; a 25 kW system is about Rs 10-14 lakh).
- The electricity generated is free to you thereafter, apart from maintenance.
- You claim **accelerated depreciation (~40% p.a.)** and **GST input credit (12%)**.

**Best for:** Profitable SMEs with taxable income, a stable premises they own or hold long-term, and appetite to deploy capital for the best long-run returns.

## OPEX / RESCO: You Buy Only The Power

Under the OPEX model - often delivered as **RESCO** (Renewable Energy Service Company) - a third-party developer installs, owns, operates and maintains the system on your roof. You sign a **power purchase agreement (PPA)** and simply pay for the units you consume at an agreed per-unit rate, typically below your grid tariff.

**How it works:**
- **Zero upfront cost.** The developer funds the entire system.
- You pay a fixed or escalating per-unit tariff (often locked in below your Rs 8-12/unit commercial rate).
- The **developer** claims depreciation and owns the asset.
- Maintenance and performance risk sit with the developer.

**Best for:** Businesses that want immediate savings with no capital outlay, don't have taxable profits to absorb depreciation, or aren't sure how long they'll stay in the building.

## The Numbers: A 25 kW Example

**CAPEX path:**
- Cost ~Rs 12 lakh; energy savings ~Rs 3.6 lakh/year (3,000 units/month at Rs 10/unit).
- Add accelerated depreciation (~Rs 1.44 lakh tax saved in year one at 30%) and GST input credit.
- Payback ~3-5 years, then 20 years of near-free power.

**OPEX/RESCO path:**
- Rs 0 upfront. If the developer charges, say, Rs 6.5/unit versus your Rs 10 grid rate, you save Rs 3.5/unit.
- On 3,000 units/month that's ~Rs 10,500/month, or ~Rs 1.26 lakh/year - immediately, with no capital at risk.
- Over the PPA you save steadily but never own the free-power upside that CAPEX unlocks after payback.

The rule of thumb: **CAPEX maximises total lifetime savings; OPEX minimises risk and upfront outlay.**

## How To Decide

Ask yourself:

1. **Do we have taxable profits?** If yes, CAPEX lets you capture ~40% accelerated depreciation. If not, that benefit is wasted - lean OPEX.
2. **Can we deploy the capital (or borrow cheaply)?** If capital is tight or better used elsewhere, OPEX preserves cash.
3. **How long will we occupy this roof?** Own the building or hold a long lease? CAPEX makes sense. Uncertain tenure? OPEX with an assignable/portable PPA is safer.
4. **Do we want to manage O&M?** CAPEX means you handle cleaning and upkeep (or an AMC). OPEX bundles it in.
5. **What's our priority - lowest cost over 25 years, or zero risk today?** That single answer usually decides it.

## Common Pitfalls To Avoid

- **PPA escalation clauses:** OPEX tariffs often rise a few percent a year. Model the full-term cost, not just year one.
- **Exit and buyout terms:** Check what happens if you sell or vacate the premises, and whether you can buy out the system.
- **Undersizing to fit a model:** Size for your daytime load first, then choose the model - not the other way around.
- **Skipping quality checks:** In both models, insist on ALMM-listed modules, BIS compliance and ~25-year performance warranties. Under OPEX, poor equipment is the developer's problem to fix - hold them to the PPA's uptime guarantees.

Whichever model you choose, note that neither is eligible for the PM Surya Ghar residential subsidy, which is for homes only. Business economics rest on tariffs, depreciation and GST instead.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the main difference between CAPEX and OPEX solar?**
CAPEX means you buy and own the system and claim tax benefits; OPEX/RESCO means a developer owns it and you just pay for the power at a lower per-unit rate with zero upfront cost.

**Which model gives more savings?**
CAPEX gives the highest lifetime savings because after a 3-5 year payback the power is near-free for 20+ years. OPEX gives smaller but immediate, risk-free savings.

**Who claims accelerated depreciation under RESCO?**
The developer, because they own the asset. If you want to claim the ~40% depreciation and 12% GST credit yourself, choose CAPEX.

**Is there any upfront cost in OPEX?**
Typically none. The developer funds the system and recovers it through the per-unit tariff over the PPA term.

**What happens if I move premises under a PPA?**
It depends on the contract. Look for buyout, relocation or assignment clauses before signing, and confirm the PPA term suits your tenure.

**Can I switch from OPEX to CAPEX later?**
Some PPAs include a buyout option after a few years. Check whether and when you can purchase the system.
