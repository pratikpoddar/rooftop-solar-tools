Rooftop solar has moved from a niche experiment to a mainstream home upgrade across India. With the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy and steadily rising electricity tariffs, lakhs of homeowners are switching. But before you invest, it helps to understand what actually happens on your roof when the sun comes up. This guide breaks it down in simple terms.

## What a rooftop solar system actually is

A rooftop solar system is a small power plant on your own roof. Solar panels capture sunlight and turn it into electricity, which powers your fans, lights, fridge, AC and everything else. When your panels make more than you need, the extra flows back to the grid. When they make less (at night or on cloudy days), you draw from the grid as usual.

For most Indian homes, this is a grid-connected (on-grid) system with no batteries. It is the cheapest, simplest and most popular setup, and it is what the government subsidy supports.

## The main parts of a rooftop solar system

A typical residential system has five components:

- **Solar panels (modules):** Mounted on your roof facing south for maximum sun. Standard panels in India are monocrystalline PERC and should be ALMM-listed and BIS-compliant.
- **Inverter:** Converts the DC electricity from panels into the AC electricity your home appliances use. This is the brain of the system.
- **Mounting structure:** Galvanised steel frames that hold panels at the right tilt and height above your roof.
- **Net meter:** A special bi-directional meter installed by your DISCOM that records both the power you export and the power you import.
- **Cables, safety gear and earthing:** Protects the system and your home from surges and faults.

## Step by step: how sunlight becomes your home's electricity

Here is the daily journey of a single unit of power:

1. **Sunlight hits the panels.** Photovoltaic cells convert light into direct current (DC) electricity. Generation is strongest between roughly 10 am and 4 pm.
2. **The inverter converts DC to AC.** Your home and the grid both run on alternating current (AC), so this step is essential.
3. **Your appliances use the power first.** The system always tries to serve your home load before anything else, cutting the units you buy from the grid.
4. **Surplus flows to the grid.** If your panels produce more than the house is using at that moment, the extra is exported through the net meter.
5. **You draw from the grid when needed.** At night or during heavy cloud, your home simply pulls power from the DISCOM like before.

As a rule of thumb, each 1kW of rooftop solar generates about 4 units per day in India. A 3kW system therefore produces roughly 330-420 units a month, enough to cover a large share of a typical urban home's bill.

## Net metering: how you get credited for extra power

Net metering is the mechanism that makes rooftop solar pay for itself. Your bi-directional meter tracks two numbers:

- **Import:** Units you buy from the grid.
- **Export:** Surplus units your system sends back.

At billing time, your DISCOM offsets exports against imports. If you export 200 units and import 250, you are billed for only the net 50 units. Any unused export credits usually carry forward to the next month, and are settled at the end of the year per your state's policy.

This is why a well-sized system can shrink your monthly bill dramatically. Eligible households under PM Surya Ghar can even receive up to 300 free units per month once the system is generating well.

## On-grid vs battery systems: a quick note

Most homeowners start with an **on-grid** system because it is the most economical and qualifies for subsidy. If you face frequent, long power cuts, you may consider a **hybrid** system with a battery for backup, but batteries add significant cost and are not subsidised. ## A real example: a 3kW home in Pune

Consider a family in Pune with a monthly bill of around Rs 4,000 and a suitable south-facing roof:

- **System size:** 3kW on-grid
- **Indicative pre-subsidy cost:** Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh
- **PM Surya Ghar central subsidy:** Rs 78,000 (the capped amount for 3kW and above)
- **Net cost after subsidy:** roughly Rs 90,000-1.3 lakh, before any state top-up
- **Generation:** about 330-420 units a month
- **Result:** Most of the family's bill is wiped out, and payback typically lands in the 3-5 year range

Over a 25-year panel life, that is well over a decade of near-free electricity after payback.

## Getting it installed: the process in brief

The path from decision to switched-on system usually looks like this:

1. Register on the national portal at pmsuryaghar.gov.in and pick an MNRE-empanelled vendor.
2. Get a site survey and a written quotation with system size, brand and price.
3. Vendor applies for DISCOM feasibility approval.
4. Installation of panels, inverter and wiring (often 1-3 days for a small home).
5. DISCOM inspects, installs the net meter and commissions the system.
6. Subsidy is credited to your bank account by Direct Benefit Transfer, typically 30-45 days after commissioning.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does rooftop solar work during power cuts?**
A standard on-grid system shuts off during a grid outage for safety, so it does not provide backup by itself. If you need power during cuts, you need a hybrid system with a battery.

**How much electricity will my system generate?**
Plan for about 4 units per day per kW. A 3kW system makes roughly 330-420 units a month, though this varies with your city, roof orientation and shading.

**Do I need a battery?**
No. Most Indian homes use battery-free on-grid systems and rely on net metering. Batteries are optional and mainly useful where power cuts are frequent and long.

**Will solar completely eliminate my electricity bill?**
Often it covers most of it, and sometimes all of it, if the system is sized to your usage. You may still pay small fixed charges to your DISCOM.

**How long do the panels last?**
Quality panels carry a performance warranty of around 25 years and degrade only about 0.5% per year, so they keep producing well past the payback period.

**Is government approval required?**
Yes. Your DISCOM must approve the connection and install a net meter. Using an MNRE-empanelled vendor with ALMM-listed modules keeps you eligible for the subsidy.
