# Rooftop Solar for Small Businesses in India: A Practical Guide

For a small business, electricity is often one of the largest controllable overheads, and it only goes up. Rooftop solar turns that rising cost into a fixed, low, and partly tax-deductible one. This practical guide covers what an Indian SME needs to know in 2026: what it costs, the tax angle, how fast it pays back, and how to get it done right.

## Why solar makes sense for SMEs

Small businesses are, in many ways, ideal solar candidates:

- **Daytime consumption** - shops, clinics, workshops, and offices run during sunlight hours, so most generation is self-consumed at the highest value.
- **Higher commercial tariffs** - commercial rates are typically higher than residential, so every unit solar offsets is worth more.
- **Tax benefits** - businesses can claim accelerated depreciation on solar assets, which households cannot.
- **A visible cost cut** - lower bills flow straight to the bottom line and improve margins.

## What it costs in 2026

Commercial rooftop solar is generally cheaper per watt than residential because systems are larger. Indicative 2026 pricing:

- **Commercial turnkey cost:** about Rs 38-50 per Wp (Rs 38,000-50,000 per kW).
- **Residential turnkey cost (for very small premises):** about Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW.
- **GST:** solar modules and systems currently attract 12% GST, which a registered business may be able to set off as input tax credit depending on its situation.
- **Net-metering/DISCOM fees:** roughly Rs 3,000-8,000, and up to about Rs 15,000 for a three-phase connection (common for commercial premises).

A 10kW commercial system, for instance, typically lands around Rs 4.5-6.5 lakh before tax benefits.

## The tax advantage: accelerated depreciation

This is where SMEs pull ahead of homeowners. Under Indian income-tax rules, businesses can claim **accelerated depreciation of about 40% on solar assets** (in addition to normal depreciation), letting you write down a large part of the system's cost against taxable profit early on.

For a profitable business, this depreciation benefit can offset a meaningful share of the investment through tax saved, shortening the effective payback considerably. Because the exact benefit depends on your profit, tax rate, and the timing of installation, confirm the numbers with your chartered accountant before you commit.

Note: the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana subsidy (Rs 30,000-78,000) is a **residential** scheme, so most commercial premises rely on tax benefits rather than that central subsidy.

## Sizing a system for your business

Match capacity to your daytime load and roof area:

1. **Check your bills.** Add up monthly units and note your commercial tariff per unit.
2. **Estimate size.** At about 4 units per day per kW (roughly 120 units/month per kW), a shop using 1,200 units/month needs roughly 10kW.
3. **Confirm roof space.** Budget for the panel area plus walkways and shading clearance.
4. **Prioritise self-consumption.** Size to what you use during the day rather than for maximum export, since commercial net-metering export rates are often below your retail tariff.

### Worked example: a 10kW system for a clinic

- **Cost:** about Rs 5 lakh turnkey (10kW at the mid of the commercial band)
- **Generation:** about 1,200 units/month (10 x 4 x 30)
- **Commercial tariff offset:** Rs 9/unit
- **Monthly saving:** roughly Rs 10,800
- **Annual saving:** about Rs 1.3 lakh, before tax benefits
- **Payback:** roughly 3-4 years on savings alone, and faster once accelerated depreciation is factored in

## Capex, loan, or RESCO?

SMEs have three common routes:

- **Capex (buy outright):** you own the system, claim depreciation, and keep all savings. Best if you have capital and taxable profit.
- **Loan/EMI:** finance the system so monthly savings offset the EMI; you still own the asset and its tax benefits.
- **RESCO/OPEX (third-party owns, you pay per unit):** no upfront cost; you buy solar power at an agreed tariff below the grid. Lower savings but zero capital and maintenance burden.

Profitable businesses that can use the tax shield usually favour capex or a loan; those wanting no upfront outlay or maintenance headache lean towards RESCO.

## Getting it done right

- **Insist on ALMM-listed modules and a reputable, MNRE-empanelled installer.** This protects warranty validity and quality.
- **Use mono PERC panels** with a performance warranty of about 25 years and roughly 0.5% annual degradation.
- **Read the quotation carefully** - confirm capacity, brand, inverter type, net-metering scope, and what after-sales service is included.
- **Plan the net-metering application** with your DISCOM early, and expect the subsidy/commissioning-style inspection process; commercial approvals can take longer than residential.
- **Schedule maintenance** - regular cleaning keeps a business system at its rated output, which matters more when generation directly cuts operating cost.

## Common pitfalls to avoid

- **Oversizing for export** where commercial export rates are low - you pay for capacity you cannot monetise well.
- **Ignoring the tax angle** - not modelling accelerated depreciation understates your true return.
- **Choosing on price alone** - the cheapest quote often uses lower-grade equipment or skips net-metering support, costing you later.
- **Skipping documentation** - keep invoices, warranties, and commissioning certificates for tax, warranty, and any future sale of the premises.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much can a small business save with rooftop solar?**
A 10kW system offsetting power at around Rs 9/unit can save roughly Rs 10,000-11,000 a month, or about Rs 1.3 lakh a year, with payback typically in 3-4 years and faster after tax benefits.

**What tax benefits can my business claim on solar?**
Businesses can claim accelerated depreciation of about 40% on solar assets (plus normal depreciation), reducing taxable profit early and effectively shortening payback. Confirm the exact benefit with your CA.

**Is there a government subsidy for commercial rooftop solar?**
The PM Surya Ghar subsidy is a residential scheme. Commercial premises generally rely on tax benefits like accelerated depreciation rather than that central subsidy, though some states have their own commercial incentives.

**What GST applies to a solar system for my business?**
Solar modules and systems currently attract 12% GST. A GST-registered business may be able to use input tax credit depending on its circumstances, so check with your accountant.

**Should I buy the system or use a RESCO model?**
Buy (capex or loan) if you have capital and taxable profit to use the depreciation benefit and want to keep all the savings. Choose RESCO/OPEX if you prefer no upfront cost or maintenance, accepting somewhat lower savings.
