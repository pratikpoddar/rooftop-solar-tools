# How to Clean Solar Panels Safely (and How Often) in India

Cleaning is the simplest, highest-value thing you can do for a rooftop solar system, and also the easiest to get wrong. Done right, it restores lost output in minutes. Done carelessly, it scratches the glass, causes water spots, or, worst of all, results in a fall from the roof.

This guide explains how to clean solar panels safely in Indian conditions, how often to do it, and the mistakes to avoid.

## Why Cleaning Matters So Much in India

India's dust, pollen, pollution, and bird droppings settle on panels and form a film that blocks sunlight. In dusty regions and polluted cities, this can cut output by **15-25%**.

On a 3 kW system that should generate 330-420 units a month, a 20% loss means losing roughly 70-80 units every month. Cleaning is essentially free electricity you are otherwise throwing away.

Panels degrade naturally by only about 0.5% per year, so the difference between a clean and a dirty panel is almost always dirt, not age.

## How Often Should You Clean?

There is no single answer; it depends on your location and season:

- **Dusty regions (Rajasthan, parts of Gujarat, Delhi-NCR):** every 2-4 weeks in the dry season
- **Average urban homes:** monthly in the dry season
- **After a dust storm or construction nearby:** immediately
- **High-pollution cities:** more often, as grime builds faster
- **During the monsoon:** rarely, because rain rinses the panels for you

A quick rule: if a glance at the panels shows a visible layer of dust, or your generation has quietly dropped on clear days, it is time to clean.

## The Best Time of Day to Clean

Clean **early morning or late evening**, never in the middle of a hot day.

- Panels are hot at midday, and cold water on hot glass risks thermal stress or cracking.
- Cleaning before the sun is strong also means you are not working on a scorching roof.
- Early morning dew often loosens dust, making cleaning easier.

## What You Need

- A **soft brush** or **microfibre cloth** on an extendable pole
- **Plain water** (ideally low in minerals to avoid spots)
- A bucket or a low-pressure hose
- Non-slip footwear and, for sloped or high roofs, a safety harness

You do not need special chemicals for routine cleaning. Water and a soft brush handle most dust.

## Step-by-Step: How to Clean Safely

1. **Switch off if advised.** For a thorough clean, some installers recommend turning the system off first; check your manual. For a light rinse, this is usually not required.
2. **Rinse first.** Gently wet the panels to loosen dust before you touch the surface, so grit does not scratch the glass.
3. **Brush gently.** Use the soft brush or microfibre cloth with light pressure, moving across the panel. Never scrub hard or use abrasive pads.
4. **Tackle stubborn spots.** For bird droppings or sticky grime, let water soak them, then wipe gently. A little mild soap can help; rinse fully afterwards.
5. **Final rinse.** Rinse off all residue with clean water.
6. **Dry if hard water.** In hard-water areas, wipe with a microfibre cloth or squeegee to prevent mineral spots that themselves block light.

## Dealing with Hard Water

Much of India has hard water, which leaves white mineral deposits as it dries. These spots reduce output over time.

- Use a final wipe-down with a clean cloth or squeegee
- Where possible, use filtered, RO reject, or soft water for the final rinse
- Avoid letting water dry on its own on the panel surface in strong sun

## Safety First, Always

More people are hurt cleaning panels than by the panels themselves.

- **Never** clean a wet, mossy, or steeply sloped roof without secure footing and a harness.
- **Never** use a high-pressure jet washer; it can force water past the seals.
- **Never** stand or lean on the panels; the glass and cells can crack.
- **Never** clean during rain or when lightning is possible.
- If your roof is high, steep, or hard to reach safely, hire a professional cleaning service.

## When to Hire a Professional

Consider professional cleaning if:

- Your array is on a tall or difficult roof
- You have a large system (many panels)
- You prefer it bundled into an Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC), which for a home system typically costs around Rs 1,500-5,000 per year including scheduled cleaning visits

A professional brings the right poles, deionised water systems, and safety gear, and can spot faults while cleaning.

## Common Cleaning Mistakes

- Cleaning at midday with cold water on hot glass
- Using detergent or abrasive brushes that scratch the anti-reflective coating
- Using a pressure washer
- Letting hard water dry into spots
- Ignoring safety on a sloped roof
- Cleaning too rarely and living with 20% lower output

## A Quick Worked Example

A Delhi homeowner with a 3 kW system noticed generation had fallen from about 12 units a day to 9.5 on clear days, a 20% drop, during the dry, dusty pre-monsoon weeks. A 20-minute early-morning rinse and soft-brush clean restored output to around 12 units. At that rate, monthly cleaning easily pays for itself in recovered units.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How often should I clean solar panels in India?**
Monthly in the dry season for most homes, every 2-4 weeks in dusty regions, and immediately after dust storms. During the monsoon, rain usually cleans them for you.

**What is the best time of day to clean solar panels?**
Early morning or late evening, when the panels are cool. Avoid midday, because cold water on hot glass can cause thermal stress.

**Can I use tap water to clean solar panels?**
Yes for routine cleaning, but in hard-water areas wipe the panels dry or use softer water for the final rinse to avoid mineral spots that block light.

**Should I use soap or special chemicals?**
Plain water and a soft brush are enough for most dust. A little mild soap helps with sticky grime, but rinse thoroughly and never use abrasive cleaners.

**Is it safe to clean the panels myself?**
Only if your roof is flat and easy to reach safely. Never use a harness-free approach on a sloped or high roof, and never a pressure washer. When in doubt, hire a professional.

**Does rain clean solar panels well enough?**
Rain helps a lot during the monsoon, but it does not remove bird droppings or sticky grime, and dust returns quickly once the rains end.
