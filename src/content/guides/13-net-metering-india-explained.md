# Net Metering in India Explained: Rules, Benefits, and How to Apply

Net metering is the mechanism that lets your rooftop solar system trade electricity with the grid, and it is one of the biggest reasons rooftop solar makes financial sense in India. Without it, surplus power your panels generate during the day would simply be wasted. With it, that surplus is credited against the electricity you draw at night. This guide explains how net metering works, the rules, the fees, and how to apply.

## What Is Net Metering?

Net metering uses a **bidirectional (net) meter** that measures electricity flowing in both directions:

- **Export:** power your solar system sends to the grid when generation exceeds your use (typically midday).
- **Import:** power you draw from the grid when your panels aren't producing enough (evenings, cloudy days, monsoon).

At the end of the billing cycle, your DISCOM bills you only on the **net** difference. If you export more than you import, the surplus usually carries forward as a credit to future bills.

## Why Net Metering Matters

Solar generation peaks during the day, but home consumption often peaks in the morning and evening. Net metering bridges this timing mismatch:

- **No expensive battery needed.** The grid acts as your "virtual battery," storing your daytime surplus as credit.
- **Bills fall to near zero.** Combined with the right system size, net metering is how eligible households reach up to 300 free units/month.
- **You are paid (in credit) for what you produce.** Surplus generation is not wasted.

A 3kW system generates roughly 330-420 units/month (about 4 units/day per kW). If your home uses less than that during the day, the excess is exported and credited.

## Net Metering vs Gross Metering

Two models exist, and they are not the same:

- **Net metering:** You self-consume your solar power first, and only the surplus is exported and credited. This is the most common and usually most beneficial for residential consumers.
- **Gross metering:** All your generation is exported to the grid at a fixed feed-in tariff, and you buy all your consumption separately. This can be less attractive when the feed-in rate is lower than the retail tariff.

Rules and thresholds vary by state and DISCOM, so confirm which model applies to your system size in your area.

## The Rules: What Varies by State and DISCOM

Net metering is regulated at the state level, so specifics differ:

- **System size caps:** Many states allow net metering up to a certain capacity (often tied to your sanctioned load) and may require net billing or gross metering above that.
- **Sanctioned load:** Your system size is usually limited relative to your connection's sanctioned load; a load enhancement may be needed for larger systems.
- **Credit carry-forward and settlement:** How surplus credits carry forward and whether/when they are settled financially varies by state.
- **Single vs three-phase:** Affects meter type and cost.

Always check your DISCOM's current net-metering policy before finalising system size.

## Fees and Costs

Net metering involves some charges outside the base system price:

- **Net meter cost:** typically Rs 3,000-8,000, and up to about Rs 15,000 for a three-phase connection.
- **Application, agreement, or inspection fees:** vary by DISCOM.
- **Possible load enhancement charges** if your sanctioned load must be increased.

Factor these into your net cost. Even so, with a residential payback of 3-5 years, these fees are a small part of the total.

## How to Apply for Net Metering

The process is integrated with the rooftop solar workflow, usually via **pmsuryaghar.gov.in** and your DISCOM:

1. **Feasibility approval:** Your DISCOM confirms your connection supports net metering and the proposed system size.
2. **Install the system:** Use an MNRE-empanelled vendor and ALMM-listed modules.
3. **Apply for the net meter:** Submit details and request a bidirectional meter and inspection.
4. **DISCOM inspection:** The utility verifies the installation and installs the net meter.
5. **Sign the net-metering agreement:** A connection agreement formalises the arrangement.
6. **Commissioning:** Once approved, the system is commissioned and net metering goes live.

After commissioning, your subsidy (via DBT) typically follows within 30-45 days.

## A Worked Example

Consider a household with a 3kW system:

- **Monthly generation:** ~350 units
- **Daytime self-consumption:** ~150 units (used directly)
- **Exported to grid:** ~200 units (credited)
- **Evening/night import from grid:** ~180 units
- **Net billed:** roughly 0 units after credits offset imports

At a tariff of ~Rs 8/unit, this household's bill drops close to zero, with any leftover export credit carried forward. That is the practical power of net metering.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a net meter?**
A bidirectional meter that measures both the electricity you export to the grid and the electricity you import, so your DISCOM bills only the net difference.

**How much does a net meter cost in India?**
Typically Rs 3,000-8,000, and up to around Rs 15,000 for a three-phase connection, plus any DISCOM application or inspection fees.

**Is net metering the same across all states?**
No. It is regulated by each state and DISCOM. Capacity caps, the choice between net and gross metering, and credit settlement rules vary, so check your local policy.

**Do I still need a battery with net metering?**
Usually not. The grid effectively stores your daytime surplus as credit, so most grid-connected homes skip batteries unless they specifically need backup during outages.

**What happens to my surplus generation?**
Under net metering, surplus is exported and credited, typically carried forward to offset future bills. Exact settlement rules depend on your state.

**How do I apply for net metering?**
Through your DISCOM, integrated with the rooftop solar process on pmsuryaghar.gov.in: feasibility approval, installation, net-meter application, inspection, agreement, and commissioning.
