# Understanding Your Electricity Bill Before Going Solar in India

Your electricity bill is the single most important document in your solar decision. It tells you exactly how much you consume, what you pay per unit, and how much you can realistically save. Before you request a single quotation, spend ten minutes learning to read it. This guide breaks down every line item you will find on a typical Indian DISCOM bill and shows how solar changes each one.

## Why the Bill Matters More Than Any Quote

Installers size systems and promise savings based on your consumption. If you understand your own bill, you can:

- Size your system correctly (avoid over- or under-buying).
- Sanity-check an installer's savings claims.
- Know your tariff slab, which determines your true per-unit saving.
- Confirm your sanctioned load supports the system you want.

## The Key Numbers to Find

### 1. Units Consumed (kWh)
Usually the headline number. It is your total energy use for the billing cycle, measured in units (1 unit = 1 kWh). Note whether the cycle is monthly or bi-monthly. Collect **12 months of bills** to capture summer AC peaks and winter geyser use. In India, **1 kW of solar generates about 4 units/day (~120 units/month)**, so your annual average consumption directly sets your system size.

### 2. Tariff Slabs (Telescopic Tariff)
Most Indian residential tariffs are **slab-based**: the price per unit rises as you consume more. For example, the first 100 units might cost one rate, the next 200 a higher rate, and units above 500 the highest rate. This matters enormously for solar because **solar offsets your most expensive (top-slab) units first**, delivering higher effective savings than the average tariff suggests.

### 3. Fixed / Demand Charges
A monthly charge based on your **sanctioned load** (in kW or kVA), not your consumption. Solar with net metering usually does **not** eliminate this line, so a small residual bill can remain even if your energy charges drop to near zero.

### 4. Sanctioned Load
The maximum load your connection is approved for. Your rooftop solar capacity is often capped at or near this figure. If you want a larger system, you may need to apply to your DISCOM for a **load enhancement**.

### 5. Other Line Items
- **Fuel surcharge / FAC / FPPCA:** A variable pass-through cost tied to fuel prices.
- **Electricity duty and taxes:** State government levies on your consumption.
- **Meter rent / service charges:** Small fixed items.
- **Subsidy adjustments:** Some states show a state subsidy or free-units credit here.

## A Worked Example

Suppose your monthly bill shows:

- **Units consumed:** 400 units
- **Energy charges (slab-based):** Rs 3,200
- **Fixed charge (3 kW sanctioned load):** Rs 300
- **Fuel surcharge + duty + taxes:** Rs 700
- **Total bill:** Rs 4,200

Your effective average tariff is Rs 4,200 ÷ 400 = **Rs 10.5 per unit**. But your top slab units may cost Rs 8-9 in energy charges alone before taxes. A **3 kW solar system** generating 330-420 units/month would offset almost all of your energy consumption. Realistically your bill could fall from Rs 4,200 to a few hundred rupees (mainly fixed charges), saving roughly **Rs 40,000-45,000 per year**. At a 3 kW pre-subsidy cost of Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh, minus a Rs 78,000 central subsidy, payback lands in the typical **3-5 year** range.

## How Net Metering Changes Your Bill

Once your on-grid solar is commissioned with **net metering**, your DISCOM installs a bi-directional meter. Your bill then reflects:

- **Import:** Units you drew from the grid (evenings, cloudy days).
- **Export:** Units your panels sent back during the day.
- **Net units:** Import minus export. You are billed on the net.

Key points to understand:

- Daytime surplus is credited against night-time usage.
- Export credits are often carried forward month to month within a settlement period.
- Export is usually valued at a lower rate than retail import, so **self-consumption is more valuable than export**.
- Fixed/demand charges typically continue regardless of net units.

## Reading the Bill to Size Your System

1. Add up 12 months of units and divide by 12 for a monthly average.
2. Divide the monthly average by ~120 to estimate the kW you need.
3. Check whether that kW fits your sanctioned load.
4. Note your top-slab rate to estimate true savings.
5. Identify fixed charges that will remain after solar.

Example: 5,400 units/year ÷ 12 = 450/month ÷ 120 ≈ **3.75 kW**, so a 3-4 kW system suits, with an eye on the 3 kW subsidy cap.

## Common Bill Misreadings to Avoid

- **Confusing bi-monthly with monthly cycles**, which doubles your apparent usage.
- **Ignoring seasonal spikes**, leading to an undersized system.
- **Assuming solar removes fixed charges**, which it usually does not.
- **Using the average tariff instead of the top-slab rate** when estimating savings.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a unit on my electricity bill?
One unit equals one kilowatt-hour (kWh). It is the standard measure of energy consumed and is what solar generation offsets.

### Will solar reduce my fixed charges too?
Generally no. Fixed/demand charges are based on sanctioned load, so a small residual bill usually remains even when energy charges fall to near zero.

### How many bills should I collect before going solar?
Ideally 12 months, so summer AC peaks and winter loads are both captured and your system is sized for the full year, not one mild month.

### Why does solar save more than my average tariff suggests?
Because slab tariffs charge more for higher consumption, solar offsets your most expensive top-slab units first, giving a higher effective saving per unit.

### What is sanctioned load and why does it matter?
It is the maximum load your connection is approved for, and your solar capacity is often capped near it. A larger system may require a load-enhancement application to your DISCOM.
