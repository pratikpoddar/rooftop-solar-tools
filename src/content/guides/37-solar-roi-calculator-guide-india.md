A solar ROI calculator turns a big, intimidating quotation into a simple question: how many rupees will this investment return, and how fast? This guide shows you exactly which numbers to feed in, how the math works, and how to sanity-check any online calculator or installer's claim using 2026 figures.

## What "ROI" means for rooftop solar

For rooftop solar, return on investment is measured three ways, and you should look at all three:

- **Payback period** - how many years until savings equal your net cost.
- **Lifetime ROI** - total savings over 25 years divided by net cost.
- **Annual return / IRR** - the yearly percentage return, useful for comparing solar against a fixed deposit or mutual fund.

A residential system in India typically shows a payback of **3-5 years**, a lifetime ROI of 300-500%, and an effective annual return well above most low-risk investments.

## The inputs a good calculator needs

Garbage in, garbage out. Any credible calculator asks for:

1. **System size (kW)** - based on your consumption and roof space.
2. **Net cost** - turnkey price minus the PM Surya Ghar subsidy and any state top-up.
3. **Expected generation** - use about 4 units per day per kW as the India baseline.
4. **Your effective tariff** - the per-unit rate solar will actually offset (usually your top slab, Rs 7-9/unit).
5. **Net-metering export rate** - what your DISCOM credits for surplus units.
6. **Tariff escalation** - assume 3-5% annual grid tariff inflation.
7. **Degradation** - about 0.5% output loss per year for mono PERC panels.
8. **O&M and inverter replacement** - light cleaning costs plus one inverter swap around year 10-12.

## The core formulas

You can reproduce any calculator with four lines of arithmetic.

- **Annual generation (units)** = system size (kW) x 4 x 365
- **Year-1 savings (Rs)** = self-consumed units x tariff + exported units x export rate
- **Net cost (Rs)** = turnkey price - central subsidy - state top-up + net-metering fee
- **Simple payback (years)** = net cost / year-1 savings

For lifetime ROI, sum each year's savings while growing the tariff by your escalation rate and shrinking generation by degradation, then subtract O&M and the inverter replacement.

## Worked example: a 5kW home system in 2026

Let's run the numbers for a Pune household consuming about 600 units a month.

- **Turnkey cost:** Rs 3 lakh (mid of the Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh band for 5kW)
- **PM Surya Ghar subsidy:** Rs 78,000 (capped at 3kW and above)
- **Net-metering fee:** Rs 5,000
- **Net cost:** Rs 3,00,000 - 78,000 + 5,000 = **Rs 2,27,000**
- **Generation:** 5 x 4 x 365 = 7,300 units/year (about 600/month)
- **Effective tariff offset:** Rs 8/unit
- **Year-1 savings:** roughly 7,300 x Rs 8 = **Rs 58,400** (assuming most units self-consumed)
- **Simple payback:** 2,27,000 / 58,400 = **about 3.9 years**

Now add tariff escalation of 4% and 0.5% degradation. Over 25 years, cumulative savings land around **Rs 20-24 lakh**, against a net cost of Rs 2.27 lakh, even after one inverter replacement of roughly Rs 25,000-40,000. That is a lifetime ROI of roughly 8-10x and an IRR comfortably in the mid-20% range.

## How to sanity-check any calculator

Online calculators and installer spreadsheets can be optimistic. Run these checks:

- **Generation not above 4.5 units/kW/day** unless you are in Rajasthan, Gujarat, or a similarly sunny zone. Anything near 5.5 is a red flag.
- **Subsidy applied correctly:** Rs 30,000 (1kW), Rs 60,000 (2kW), Rs 78,000 (3kW+). It should not scale linearly above 3kW.
- **Export rate realistic:** many states credit surplus below your retail tariff, so 100% export-value assumptions inflate returns.
- **Escalation stated:** a calculator that ignores tariff inflation understates real returns; one assuming 8-10% overstates them.
- **Costs included:** net-metering fees, cleaning, and inverter replacement should appear somewhere.

## Adjusting for your situation

- **Three-phase connection:** budget up to about Rs 15,000 for net metering instead of Rs 3,000-8,000.
- **High daytime usage** (work-from-home, ACs, pumps): self-consumption rises, so savings and IRR improve.
- **Mostly evening usage:** more surplus export at a lower rate slightly lowers ROI unless you add a battery.
- **Business use:** companies can claim accelerated depreciation of about 40% on solar assets (plus normal depreciation) and should model that tax saving separately, alongside 12% GST on the system.

## A quick DIY estimate in five minutes

1. Divide your monthly bill by your per-unit tariff to estimate monthly units.
2. Divide monthly units by 120 to get an approximate kW size.
3. Look up turnkey cost at roughly Rs 55,000-70,000/kW residential.
4. Subtract the correct PM Surya Ghar subsidy.
5. Divide net cost by your annual savings for payback.

If the answer is under five years, solar is almost certainly worth it for you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a good ROI for rooftop solar in India?**
A payback of under five years and a lifetime ROI of 3-5x are typical and considered strong. Well-utilised systems on high tariffs often pay back in about three years.

**Are online solar ROI calculators accurate?**
They are a good starting point but frequently assume optimistic generation and 100% export value. Always verify the generation figure (about 4 units/kW/day) and that the subsidy is applied correctly.

**Should I include the subsidy in my ROI math?**
Yes. Model your net cost after the PM Surya Ghar subsidy and any state top-up, since the subsidy is paid by DBT within roughly 30-45 days of commissioning and materially improves your return.

**How do I compare solar's return to an FD?**
Convert solar's savings into an effective annual return (IRR). Rooftop solar's IRR usually far exceeds fixed deposit rates, and unlike an FD, solar's returns are effectively tax-free and rise with tariffs.

**What reduces my ROI the most?**
Shading, oversizing beyond your consumption when export rates are low, and skipping cleaning. Keeping the roof clear and the system sized to your usage protects your returns.
