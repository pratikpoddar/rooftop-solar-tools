Your solar system will run for 25 years, but the installer you choose determines whether those years are smooth or a maintenance headache. In India's fast-growing market, quality varies widely between fly-by-night resellers and established EPC firms. This guide gives you 12 practical questions to ask before signing, so you pay a fair price and get a system that lasts.

## Why the Installer Matters as Much as the Panels

Panels and inverters are commodities with published specs. The installer's workmanship, documentation, and after-sales support are what actually vary. A poor installer can:

- Use non-ALMM modules that disqualify your subsidy.
- Cut corners on earthing, structure, or cabling.
- Disappear when you need warranty support.
- Mishandle your DISCOM and portal paperwork, delaying commissioning and subsidy.

Getting three quotes and asking the right questions protects a Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh (3 kW) or larger investment.

## The 12 Questions to Ask Every Installer

### 1. Are you MNRE-empanelled for my DISCOM?
For the PM Surya Ghar central subsidy, you **must** use an MNRE-empanelled vendor listed for your DISCOM on pmsuryaghar.gov.in. Ask for their listing and verify it yourself.

### 2. Are the panels ALMM-listed and BIS-compliant?
Only **ALMM-listed modules** qualify for the subsidy and meet quality norms. Ask for the exact make, model, and wattage, and confirm they are BIS-certified. Standard 2026 residential panels are mono PERC of 540-575 Wp.

### 3. What is the panel and inverter warranty?
Expect around a **25-year performance warranty** on panels (with roughly 0.5% per year degradation) and a separate product warranty. Inverters typically carry 5-10 year warranties. Get warranty terms in writing, not verbally.

### 4. Who handles the subsidy and net-metering paperwork?
A good installer manages your portal application, DISCOM feasibility, net-metering application, and inspection coordination end to end. Confirm this is included, not an extra you must chase.

### 5. Can I see the full itemised quotation?
Insist on a line-item breakdown: panels, inverter, mounting structure, cabling, safety gear, net-metering fees, installation, GST, and any DISCOM charges. Vague lump sums hide corners being cut.

### 6. What mounting structure and earthing do you use?
Ask for galvanised (GI) structure specs, wind-load rating, proper earthing, and lightning/surge protection. This is where cheap installers cut costs invisibly.

### 7. How many installations have you completed, and can I see references?
Ask for the number of projects in your city and 2-3 recent customer references you can call. Local track record beats glossy brochures.

### 8. What is the total timeline and payment schedule?
A reliable vendor gives a written timeline (typically 4-8 weeks end to end) and a staged payment plan tied to milestones, not full payment upfront.

### 9. What after-sales and maintenance support do you offer?
Ask about response times for faults, annual maintenance options, cleaning guidance, and whether they have a local service team. A 25-year asset needs a vendor who will still be reachable.

### 10. Is there a monitoring system?
Modern inverters offer app-based monitoring so you can track daily generation and spot underperformance. Confirm it is included and that they will help you read it.

### 11. What generation do you guarantee, and on what basis?
A credible installer estimates output using your city's irradiance (around 4 units/kW/day nationally, 330-420 units/month for 3 kW) and site-specific shading, not an inflated brochure figure. Be wary of anyone promising far above the norm.

### 12. What happens if the DISCOM inspection fails?
Confirm they will rectify any issues at no extra cost until commissioning succeeds. This protects you from being stuck between vendor and DISCOM.

## Compare Quotes on Value, Not Just Price

The cheapest quote is rarely the best. Residential turnkey pricing in 2026 runs about **Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW**. A quote far below this range usually signals non-ALMM panels, thin cabling, a weak structure, or missing paperwork support. Compare on:

- Equipment quality (ALMM panels, reputable inverter).
- Warranty terms and installer longevity.
- Inclusion of all paperwork and net-metering fees.
- After-sales reputation.

### A Worked Comparison

Suppose you get three 3 kW quotes:

- **Vendor A:** Rs 1.5 lakh, non-empanelled, unnamed panels, no paperwork help. Disqualifies you from the Rs 78,000 subsidy, so net cost is Rs 1.5 lakh.
- **Vendor B:** Rs 1.9 lakh, MNRE-empanelled, ALMM mono PERC, handles all paperwork. After subsidy, net cost is about Rs 1.12 lakh.
- **Vendor C:** Rs 2.1 lakh, same quality as B plus a stronger AMC and monitoring.

Vendor B or C is clearly better value than the "cheap" Vendor A once the subsidy and reliability are factored in.

## Red Flags to Watch During the Conversation

- Reluctance to name panel make/model or show ALMM listing.
- Demanding full payment upfront.
- No written warranty or timeline.
- Guaranteeing generation far above 4 units/kW/day.
- No local references or service presence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I have to use an MNRE-empanelled installer?
For the central PM Surya Ghar subsidy, yes. Non-empanelled installers may be cheaper but make you ineligible for the Rs 30,000-78,000 central subsidy.

### How do I verify an installer is legitimate?
Check their empanelment on pmsuryaghar.gov.in for your DISCOM, confirm ALMM panel models, ask for local references, and insist on written warranties and an itemised quote.

### How many quotes should I get?
At least three. Compare them on equipment quality, warranties, paperwork support, and after-sales, not just headline price.

### What warranty should I expect?
Around a 25-year performance warranty on panels with about 0.5% annual degradation, plus a separate product warranty and a 5-10 year inverter warranty.

### Is the cheapest installer a bad idea?
Often, yes. A price well below Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW usually means non-ALMM panels or cut corners that cost more in lost subsidy and repairs later.
