If your monthly electricity bill has crept past Rs 2,000 and keeps climbing, rooftop solar is probably the single highest-return upgrade you can make to your home. But "you'll save a lot" is not a number. This guide gives you real 2026 figures: how many units a system generates, what that removes from your bill, and how much you keep over 25 years.

## The short answer

A well-sized residential rooftop solar system in India typically pays for itself in **3 to 5 years** and then delivers free-to-cheap power for another 20 years. For a family paying Rs 4,000-6,000 a month, a 3kW system can cut the electricity bill by 70-90% and save **Rs 4-6 lakh over its lifetime**.

Your actual savings depend on three things:

- **How much you generate** (system size and your city's sunlight)
- **How much you consume** and your tariff slab
- **Your net-metering policy** (what your DISCOM pays for surplus units)

## How much a system actually generates

The reliable rule of thumb across most of India is about **4 units (kWh) per day for every 1kW** of installed capacity, averaged over the year.

- 1kW - roughly 120 units/month
- 2kW - roughly 240 units/month
- 3kW - roughly 330-420 units/month
- 5kW - roughly 550-650 units/month

Sunnier states (Rajasthan, Gujarat) sit at the higher end; monsoon-heavy regions and shaded roofs at the lower end. Modern mono PERC panels degrade only about 0.5% per year, so a system still produces around 88-90% of its original output at year 25.

## Turning units into rupee savings

Savings come from two sources: units you use directly (avoiding grid purchase) and surplus units you export under net metering.

Every unit you self-consume saves you your **highest tariff slab**, which in most cities is Rs 7-9 per unit including fixed charges and duties. Because Indian tariffs are slabbed, solar shaves off your most expensive units first, which is why the savings are larger than a flat rate suggests.

### Worked example: a 3kW system in Bangalore

- **Cost:** about Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh before subsidy
- **PM Surya Ghar subsidy:** Rs 78,000 (the capped amount for 3kW and above)
- **Net cost:** roughly Rs 90,000-1.3 lakh
- **Generation:** about 360 units/month
- **Old bill:** Rs 5,500/month (about 550 units)
- **New bill:** about Rs 800-1,200/month after solar offsets most consumption
- **Monthly saving:** about Rs 4,300-4,700
- **Annual saving:** roughly Rs 52,000-56,000

At that rate the net investment is recovered in about **2 to 2.5 years**, and every year after is close to pure saving.

## What subsidy does to your numbers

The central **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** subsidy directly reduces your upfront cost and therefore your payback period:

- 1kW - Rs 30,000
- 2kW - Rs 60,000
- 3kW and above - Rs 78,000 (capped)

The slab structure is Rs 30,000 per kW for the first 2kW, Rs 18,000 per kW for the 2-3kW band, and nothing above 3kW. Eligible households can also receive **up to 300 free units per month**. Several states add their own top-up, which lowers your net cost even further. The subsidy is paid by DBT to your bank account roughly 30-45 days after your DISCOM inspects and commissions the system.

## The savings the calculator misses

Two effects make solar better than a simple payback figure:

- **Tariff inflation as a hedge.** Grid electricity in India has risen roughly 3-5% a year. Every future hike increases what your panels save, so your savings compound over time even though your generation stays flat.
- **Nearly free power for 20 years.** After payback, your only cost is occasional cleaning and one inverter replacement (roughly year 10-12). A 3kW system delivering Rs 50,000+ of value each year adds up to Rs 4-6 lakh across its life.

## What eats into savings (be realistic)

- **Net-metering and DISCOM fees:** a one-time Rs 3,000-8,000, or up to about Rs 15,000 for three-phase connections.
- **Low export rates:** some states credit surplus at a wholesale rate rather than your retail tariff, so oversizing beyond your consumption gives diminishing returns.
- **Shading and dust:** an unshaded, regularly cleaned roof is essential to hit the 4-units-per-kW figure.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much can I realistically save each month with a 3kW system?**
For a household previously paying Rs 5,000-5,500, expect the bill to drop to roughly Rs 800-1,200, a saving of about Rs 4,000-4,700 per month, assuming you use most of the daytime generation.

**Will solar ever make my bill zero?**
It can get very close. Fixed charges and a small always-on grid dependence (nights, cloudy days) usually leave a small bill unless you add a battery. Most homeowners aim to zero out the expensive top slabs rather than the entire bill.

**How long until I break even?**
Residential payback is typically 3-5 years, and often faster after the PM Surya Ghar subsidy. Larger, well-utilised systems on higher tariffs break even at the lower end of that range.

**Do savings shrink as panels age?**
Only slightly. Mono PERC panels lose about 0.5% output per year, so after 25 years you still generate close to 88-90% of the original. Meanwhile tariffs keep rising, so real rupee savings usually grow.

**Does the subsidy make a big difference to savings?**
Yes. The Rs 78,000 subsidy for a 3kW system can cut your payback by roughly a year and lifts your overall return meaningfully.
