Pune is one of India's most solar-friendly big cities. Its dry, sunny climate, large stock of independent bungalows and row houses, and MSEDCL's established net-metering process make rooftop solar a straightforward, high-return upgrade. Whether you own a home in Kothrud, Baner, Wagholi or a shop in the older city, this guide covers 2026 costs, the PM Surya Ghar subsidy, net metering and what to expect.

## Why Pune is a strong market for rooftop solar

- **Excellent sun.** Pune's climate delivers steady generation of roughly 4 units per day per kW, with fewer humid, overcast stretches than coastal cities.
- **Roof availability.** Bungalows, row houses and twin bungalows across the suburbs offer open, shadow-free terraces.
- **Rising MSEDCL tariffs.** Higher slab rates make each self-generated unit more valuable.
- **Central subsidy.** PM Surya Ghar cuts upfront cost for residential users significantly.

A 3 kW system in Pune commonly generates about 330-420 units a month, covering most of a typical household's usage.

## What rooftop solar costs in Pune (2026)

Turnkey residential systems in 2026 cost about Rs 55,000-70,000 per kW (roughly Rs 40-55 per watt) with ALMM-listed mono PERC panels. Indicative pre-subsidy costs:

- **1 kW:** Rs 60,000-80,000
- **2 kW:** Rs 1.1-1.5 lakh
- **3 kW:** Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh
- **5 kW:** Rs 2.5-3.5 lakh
- **10 kW:** Rs 4.5-6.5 lakh

Small-business and commercial systems typically come in nearer Rs 38-50 per watt because they are larger. Add roughly Rs 3,000-8,000 for net metering and DISCOM charges, or up to about Rs 15,000 for three-phase.

## PM Surya Ghar subsidy for Pune homes

The central subsidy applies across Maharashtra, including Pune:

- **1 kW:** Rs 30,000
- **2 kW:** Rs 60,000
- **3 kW and above:** Rs 78,000 (capped)

That is Rs 30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW plus Rs 18,000 for the third kW, with nothing above 3 kW. Register at pmsuryaghar.gov.in, use an MNRE-empanelled vendor with ALMM-listed modules, and the subsidy reaches your bank by DBT about 30-45 days after MSEDCL commissions the system. Eligible households can also access up to 300 free units a month.

## Net metering with MSEDCL

Pune is served by MSEDCL (Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Company). Under net metering, regulated by MERC:

- Daytime solar exports are credited against the units you draw from the grid, and you pay only the net.
- System size is generally tied to your sanctioned load, so choose capacity accordingly.
- Surplus credits typically carry forward within the settlement period rather than being paid in cash.
- Three-phase connections attract slightly higher metering and processing charges.

Apply for net metering together with your subsidy application; MSEDCL conducts a feasibility check, then inspects and installs the bidirectional meter before commissioning.

## Sizing and roof feasibility

- **Space needed.** About 80-100 sq ft of unshaded roof per kW, so a 3 kW system needs roughly 250-300 sq ft.
- **Match to your bill.** Size the system to your monthly consumption; there is little value in exporting far more than you use given carry-forward credit rules.
- **Shading.** Keep panels clear of water tanks, parapet shadows and nearby trees.
- **Structure.** Standard RCC terraces easily support the lightweight mounting.

## A worked example: 3 kW home in Baner

Consider a bungalow in Baner on MSEDCL supply consuming around 400 units a month:

- **Pre-subsidy cost:** about Rs 1.85 lakh
- **PM Surya Ghar subsidy:** Rs 78,000
- **Net outlay:** about Rs 1.07 lakh (plus ~Rs 5,000 net metering)
- **Generation:** roughly 360 units a month
- **Bill offset:** at Pune's slab rates, savings of around Rs 2,500-3,200 a month, or Rs 30,000-38,000 a year

Payback lands around 3-4 years. With a 25-year performance warranty and just ~0.5% annual degradation, the system keeps generating cheap electricity long after it has recovered its cost.

## Choosing the right installer

- Confirm **ALMM-listed** panels and **BIS-compliant** inverters and balance-of-system.
- Use an **MNRE-empanelled vendor** to remain subsidy-eligible.
- Ask for an itemised quote covering panel brand, inverter, warranties and net-metering handling.
- Verify the promised **25-year performance warranty** and a written maintenance plan.
- Avoid quotes far below market rates, which often cut corners on panel quality or omit DISCOM liaison.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which DISCOM covers Pune?**
MSEDCL distributes power across Pune and handles your net-metering application and meter installation.

**How much can a 3 kW system save in Pune?**
Around 330-420 units a month, worth roughly Rs 2,500-3,200 monthly or Rs 30,000-38,000 a year at prevailing MSEDCL slab rates.

**What is the net cost of a 3 kW system after subsidy?**
Pre-subsidy Rs 1.65-2.1 lakh, less the Rs 78,000 subsidy, leaves a net outlay near Rs 1 lakh plus small net-metering charges.

**How long does the whole process take?**
Installation is quick, but MSEDCL feasibility, meter change and commissioning add a few weeks before the subsidy is disbursed.

**Do surplus units earn cash?**
Under MSEDCL net metering, surplus generation is usually carried forward as credit within the settlement period rather than paid out in cash.

**Is a battery necessary in Pune?**
For grid-connected homes with net metering, a battery is optional and mainly useful if you experience frequent power cuts.
